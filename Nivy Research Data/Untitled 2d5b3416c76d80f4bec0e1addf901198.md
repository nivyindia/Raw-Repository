Excellent question. This is a classic challenge in organizational design for holding companies, conglomerates, and multi-service brands. The key principle is shared services vs. business-specific functions.

Here’s a breakdown of what typically remains the same (centralized/at the brand level) and what changes (decentralized/at the company or service level).

---

Departments/Functions That Typically Remain the Same (Centralized/Core Brand Level)

These are often consolidated to ensure consistency, leverage economies of scale, and protect the core brand equity.

1. Brand Strategy & Marketing (Core):
· What stays central: Overall brand identity, core brand values, master brand messaging, major brand campaigns, brand guidelines (logo, color, voice), and reputation management.
· Why: To ensure a coherent public image and strategic direction for the entire ecosystem.
2. Finance (Strategic & Core):
· What stays central: Investor relations, group/consolidated financial reporting, treasury/cash management, mergers & acquisitions (M&A), high-level tax strategy, and internal audit.
· Why: For financial control, regulatory compliance, capital allocation, and presenting a unified financial picture to investors.
3. Legal & Compliance (Core):
· What stays central: Corporate governance, intellectual property (trademarks, patents for the brand), major litigation, group-wide compliance frameworks (e.g., anti-bribery, data privacy policy), and board matters.
· Why: To mitigate major risks and protect the entire organization's legal integrity.
4. Technology & IT Infrastructure (Shared Services):
· What stays central: Core IT infrastructure (networks, data centers), enterprise software (ERP, CRM for group use), cybersecurity strategy and core defense, and data governance policies.
· Why: Significant cost savings, security standardization, and enabling interoperability.
5. Human Resources (Strategic & Shared Services):
· What stays central: HR strategy & philosophy, leadership development/talent pipeline for senior roles, group-wide compensation & benefits frameworks, core HR systems (HRIS), and employer branding.
· Why: To cultivate a unifying culture and develop future leaders for the group.
6. Real Estate & Facilities Management (Shared Services):
· Often centralized for procurement, management of major corporate offices, and negotiating master service agreements.

---

Departments/Functions That Typically Change (Decentralized/Business Unit Level)

These are adapted to meet the specific market, customer, and operational needs of each service or company.

1. Product/Service Development & Management:
· Why it changes: A software company and a logistics company under one brand have completely different products, R&D cycles, and engineering teams. This must be owned by each business unit.
2. Sales & Business Development:
· Why it changes: Sales channels, customer profiles (B2B vs. B2C), sales cycles, and partnership models are entirely dependent on the specific service or industry.
3. Marketing (Execution & Tactical):
· What changes: Performance marketing (SEO/SEM, social ads), trade shows, promotional campaigns, product marketing, and regional/local advertising.
· Why: Execution must speak directly to the target customer segment and competitive landscape of that specific business.
4. Operations & Delivery:
· Why it changes: This is the heart of each business. A consulting firm's service delivery is nothing like a manufacturing unit's supply chain or a retail outlet's store operations.
5. Customer Service/Support:
· Why it changes: Support needs, channels (phone, chat, field service), and expertise are intimately tied to the product or service delivered.
6. Human Resources (Tactical & Business-Partner):
· What changes: Recruitment for operational roles, local onboarding, business-unit-specific performance management, and employee relations are handled within each company to stay agile and relevant.
7. Finance (Operational):
· What changes: Business unit P&L management, departmental budgeting, accounts payable/receivable for operations, and cost accounting are best managed close to the business.

---

The "Hybrid" or Matrix Model: The Most Common Reality

In practice, most successful multi-company brands use a hybrid model:

· A small, powerful central/corporate team sets strategy, policy, and guards the brand.
· Embedded teams within each business unit execute on day-to-day operations.
· "Centers of Excellence" (CoEs) at the center provide expertise, tools, and support to the business units. For example:
· A Central Digital Marketing CoE might build shared tools and best practices, but each business unit's marketing team runs its own campaigns.
· A Central HR CoE might manage the benefits platform, while HR Business Partners in each company handle local needs.

The ultimate goal is to centralize what is strategic and provides scale, and decentralize what is tactical and requires speed, specificity, and deep customer/market knowledge. The exact split is a key strategic decision that defines the balance between synergy and autonomy.
