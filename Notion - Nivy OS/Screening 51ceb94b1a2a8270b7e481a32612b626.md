# Screening

### The Golden Rule of CV Screening

**You are not looking for the *perfect* candidate; you are looking for a *red flag* elimination process and a *green flag* identification process.**

Your goal is to narrow down the pile to a manageable shortlist of candidates who are **worth interviewing.**

---

### Step 1: Preparation *Before* You See Any CV

This is the most important step. Without clear criteria, you'll be swayed by flashy CVs that don't actually fit the role.

1. **Create a "Must-Have" vs. "Nice-to-Have" List:**
    - **Must-Haves (Non-negotiable):** These are the minimum qualifications without which the candidate cannot do the job. Examples: Specific degree, 3+ years of experience in a certain field, right to work in the country, specific technical certification.
    - **Nice-to-Haves (Desirable):** Skills or experiences that would be a bonus but aren't essential. Examples: Experience with a specific software, knowledge of a second language, experience in your particular industry.
2. **Define the Core Competencies for the Role:**
    - What are the 5-7 key skills (both technical and soft) required for success in this position? Examples: Project Management, Python Programming, Client Communication, Data Analysis, Team Leadership.

### Step 2: The 60-Second "First Pass" - The Elimination Round

Your goal here is to quickly filter out the clearly unqualified applicants. Spend no more than 60 seconds per CV on this pass. Look for these **Red Flags**:

- **Fails "Must-Have" Criteria:** Clearly missing a non-negotiable requirement (e.g., the role is for a senior developer, and the candidate is a recent grad with no relevant experience).
- **Frequent Job Hopping:** A pattern of staying at jobs for less than 1-1.5 years without good reason (e.g., contracts, company acquisitions). One or two short stints are okay; a career full of them is a red flag.
- **Major Gaps in Employment:** Unexplained gaps of more than 6-12 months. (Note: Be mindful that gaps could be for valid reasons like caregiving, health, or education, but they require explanation later).
- **Spelling and Grammatical Errors:** More than one or two typos shows a lack of attention to detail. If they aren't careful with their CV, how will they be with their work?
- **Generic, "Spray-and-Pray" CV:** The CV has no mention of your company, your industry, or the specific role. It's clearly a mass-produced document sent to hundreds of jobs. A lack of a cover letter (where requested) can also indicate this.

**Action:** Create a "No" pile for CVs that hit any major red flags.

### Step 3: The 2-3 Minute "Detailed Scan" - The Qualification Round

Now, take the CVs that passed the first round and give them a closer look. You are now looking for **Green Flags**.

**A. Structure & Presentation:**

- **Is it easy to read?** Is there clear formatting, bullet points, and sensible headings? A well-organized CV often reflects an organized mind.
- **Is it achievement-oriented?** Look for action verbs and quantifiable results.

**B. The "So What?" Test for Experience:**
Don't just read job descriptions. Scrutinize their achievements. A good CV answers "So what?" for every bullet point.

- **Weak:** "Responsible for social media management."
- **Strong (Green Flag):** "Grew social media engagement by 45% over 6 months by implementing a new content strategy."

Look for patterns of:

- **Impact:** Increased revenue, reduced costs, improved efficiency, grew followers, etc.
- **Initiative:** "Spearheaded," "Initiated," "Founded," "Led."
- **Skills Application:** Evidence that they used the skills you need. If you need Python, look for a project or achievement where they used Python to solve a problem.

**C. Relevance and Progression:**

- Does their career path show logical progression and increasing responsibility?
- Is their experience relevant to *your* industry and *your* specific role? A candidate from a different industry who can draw clear parallels in their cover letter or CV summary is a strong contender.

### Step 4: Ranking and Shortlisting

After the detailed scan, you should have a "Maybe" pile. Now, rank them.

1. **Score them against your "Must-Have" and "Nice-to-Have" list.** Give points for each requirement met.
2. **Prioritize CVs that tell a compelling story.** The best CVs are not just lists; they tell the story of a candidate who is a perfect fit for *your* problem. They have the skills and have proven they can deliver the results you need.
3. **Look for signs of the soft skills you value:** While hard to gauge from a CV, look for clues:
    - **Hardworking:** Quantifiable achievements, side projects, continuous learning.
    - **Leadership/Teamwork:** Words like "coordinated," "collaborated with X department," "led a team of Y."
    - **Communication:** Well-written CV, clear summaries, mentions of presenting, reporting, or writing.

### Special Case: How to Screen a Fresher's CV

Since they lack work experience, you must look elsewhere:

- **Academic Projects:** Do they describe their role and the outcome in a project? This is their equivalent of work experience.
- **Internships & Volunteering:** This is gold. Treat it like a real job. What did they accomplish?
- **Extracurricular Activities:** Club leadership, sports team captain, organizing events. This shows initiative, teamwork, and time management.
- **Skills & Certifications:** Have they gone beyond their curriculum to learn relevant tools or get certified?
- **Online Presence:** A link to a well-maintained GitHub (for tech), a portfolio (for design/writing), or a professional LinkedIn profile can be very revealing.

### Summary: A Quick Checklist for "Proper CVs"

| What to Look For | Green Flags (Yes!) | Red Flags (No!) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Relevance** | Skills & experience directly match your "Must-Haves". | Generic CV with no obvious link to the role. |
| **Achievements** | Uses numbers and metrics to show impact (% growth, $ saved, etc.). | Only lists job duties with no results. |
| **Progression** | Shows increasing responsibility over time. | Unexplained gaps or frequent job-hopping. |
| **Attention to Detail** | Clean, error-free, and professionally formatted. | Spelling/grammar mistakes, sloppy layout. |
| **Story** | CV summary and experience tell a coherent story of a qualified candidate. | Disjointed experience that doesn't add up. |

By following this structured process, you move from a subjective "gut feeling" to an objective, efficient, and effective method for selecting the CVs that are truly worth your time.
