# Correct way to plan and execute

Short answer: **Make it rough → execute fast → improve continuously.**

Trying to make a plan 100% accurate *before* execution is usually a trap.

Here’s the clear thinking behind it 👇

---

## 1. Why “100% accurate planning” fails

- Reality always introduces **unknown variables**
- You can’t predict:
    - Market response
    - Human behavior
    - Execution bottlenecks
    - Hidden constraints
- Time spent perfecting = **lost opportunity**
- Most plans break **on Day 1 of execution**

> A perfect plan that’s never executed is useless.
> 

---

## 2. The smarter approach (used by real operators)

### **Phase 1: Rough but intelligent plan (70% clarity)**

Before starting, you must define:

- 🎯 **Goal** (clear & measurable)
- 🧭 **Direction** (what to do first)
- 📌 **Constraints** (money, time, manpower)
- 🔁 **Feedback loop** (how you’ll measure & adjust)

❌ Don’t overthink:

- Edge cases
- Rare scenarios
- Future optimizations

---

### **Phase 2: Execute fast**

- Start with the **simplest version**
- Get **real-world feedback**
- Notice:
    - What’s working
    - What’s breaking
    - What’s slowing things down

> Execution reveals truths that planning never will.
> 

---

### **Phase 3: Improve while working (real optimization)**

Now:

- Fix bottlenecks
- Add systems
- Handle scenarios **only when they appear**
- Upgrade SOPs

This is where accuracy actually comes from — **data, not imagination**.

---

## 3. Rule of thumb (very important)

- **Plan = direction**
- **Execution = truth**
- **Iteration = accuracy**

If you wait for 100% certainty:

- Someone else executes at 60%
- Learns faster
- Wins earlier

---

## 4. When should you plan deeply?

Only when:

- ❗ Cost of failure is very high (legal, safety, capital-heavy)
- ❗ Changes are expensive or irreversible

Even then → plan 80%, not 100%.

---

## 5. Brutal truth

> People who demand perfect plans usually fear execution.
> 

Action-takers:

- Start imperfect
- Correct publicly
- Scale quietly
