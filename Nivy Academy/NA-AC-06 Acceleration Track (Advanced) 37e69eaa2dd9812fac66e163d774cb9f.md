# NA-AC-06 | Acceleration Track

## For Experienced Freelancers & Working Professionals

---

## Who Is This For?

The Acceleration Track is designed for students who:

- Already have 6+ months of freelancing or marketing experience
- Understand the basics of outreach but aren't converting consistently
- Want to scale from inconsistent income to ₹50K–₹2L/month (or equivalent internationally)
- Are currently employed and want to transition to higher-paying global clients

---

## 🚪 Entry Diagnostic — Admission Requirement

**Status: Mandatory before enrolment.** Because this track moves fast and assumes prior experience, every applicant must pass this diagnostic before being admitted. This protects both the student (wrong-fit students get redirected to the Foundation Track instead of struggling) and the cohort (keeps the pace appropriate for everyone).

### Step 1 — Experience Verification (Pass/Fail, evidence required)

Applicant must submit evidence of **at least one** of the following:

- 6+ months of freelance/agency work history (screenshots of past client conversations, invoices, or a portfolio)
- A current job in digital marketing, sales, or VA work (LinkedIn profile + offer letter or pay slip)
- Completion of Nivy's own Foundation Track (automatic pass on this step)

If an applicant cannot provide evidence for any of these, they are redirected to the Foundation Track. No exceptions — this is the single most common reason for Acceleration Track dropouts.

### Step 2 — Written Diagnostic (15 questions, 30 minutes, pass mark 10/15)

A mix of scenario-based and knowledge questions covering outreach, pricing, objection handling, and tool familiarity. Sample questions:

1. A prospect replies "We already have an agency, but thanks." What is your best next move?
    
    A) Apologise and stop contacting them B) Ask what's missing from their current agency relationship and offer a free audit to find gaps C) Offer a 50% discount immediately D) Mark as dead lead and move on
    
2. You're quoting a US-based e-commerce client for ongoing marketing support. Which pricing model signals the most professionalism for ongoing work?
    
    A) One-time flat fee with no scope B) Hourly with no minimum C) Monthly retainer with a defined scope and deliverables D) "Pay what you think it's worth"
    
3. What is the primary purpose of a CRM in a freelance outreach business?
    
    A) To store passwords B) To track every lead's status, last contact date, and next action so nothing falls through the cracks C) To send invoices only D) It has no real purpose for freelancers
    
4. A client asks for a 30% discount mid-contract with no change in scope. What's the professional response?
    
    A) Agree immediately to keep the client happy B) End the contract without discussion C) Acknowledge the request, explain the value already being delivered, and offer alternatives (reduced scope, payment terms) instead of a blanket discount D) Ignore the message
    
5. Which of these is a red flag when evaluating a potential client during a discovery call?
    
    A) They ask clarifying questions about your process B) They have a clear budget range C) They say "We've fired the last three agencies, but it was always their fault" D) They want a written proposal

## Week-by-Week Breakdown

### 📅 Block 1 (Weeks 1–2): Audit & Positioning Reset

**Goal:** Diagnose exactly why the student's current pipeline isn't converting, then rebuild their positioning so they show up as a premium consultant, not a commodity freelancer.

**Tools used this block:** LinkedIn, Google Sheets/Notion (for the audit tracker), Canva

#### Week 1 — Diagnostic Audit

**Day 1 — Pipeline & Income Audit**

Map every client/lead the student has worked with or contacted in the last 6 months: source, outcome, value, and why it did or didn't convert. Most students discover their "pipeline" is actually a handful of unstructured contacts with no follow-up system. Lesson covers how to build a simple audit tracker (columns: Date, Source, Contact, Status, Value, Notes) and why looking at raw numbers — not feelings — reveals the real leak (usually: no follow-up, or pricing too low to be taken seriously, or wrong audience).

*Task:* Build your Pipeline Audit Tracker in Notion or Sheets and populate it with every lead/client from the last 6 months (minimum 10 entries, or all if fewer than 10).

**Day 2 — Niche & Offer Audit**

Review what the student currently offers and to whom. Lesson covers the difference between a "skill" (e.g. "I do social media") and an "offer" (e.g. "I help DTC skincare brands turn cold traffic into repeat customers through a 90-day content + paid ads system"). Most experienced freelancers are stuck because their offer is too broad — trying to serve everyone means standing out to no one.

*Task:* Write your current offer statement as it stands today, then identify 2 narrower niche/offer combinations you could pivot toward based on your strongest past results.

**Day 3 — Pricing Audit**

Review every rate the student has charged in the last 6 months against international benchmarks (US/UK freelance rates for the same service, sourced from Upwork/industry reports). Lesson covers why underpricing damages positioning — clients often read low prices as low confidence or low quality, not as a bargain.

*Task:* List your last 5 rates charged vs. the equivalent US/UK market rate for that service. Calculate the average gap (%).

**Day 4 — Outreach Channel Audit**

Review where leads are currently coming from (or not coming from). Lesson covers the most common outreach failure pattern for experienced freelancers: relying on referrals/inbound only, with zero active outbound system, leading to feast-or-famine income.

*Task:* List every channel you've used for finding clients in the last 6 months and how many leads/clients each produced. Identify which channel is completely unused that shouldn't be.

**Day 5 — Audit Synthesis & Gap Report**

Combine Days 1–4 into a single "Gap Report": the single biggest leak in the student's current business (usually one of: no follow-up system, offer too broad, pricing too low, or no active outbound channel).

*Task:* Submit your Gap Report (1 page) identifying your #1 leak and a one-sentence hypothesis for fixing it. This becomes the brief for Week 2.

#### Week 2 — Repositioning

**Day 6 — Personal Brand Repositioning Framework**

Lesson introduces the "Specialist Positioning Framework": Niche + Outcome + Mechanism (e.g. "I help [niche] achieve [outcome] through [mechanism/system]"). Covers how to choose a mechanism that's defensible (a named system, not just "hard work") and how to validate a new positioning against the student's actual track record — don't reposition into a niche with zero proof of results.

*Task:* Draft 3 versions of your new positioning statement using the framework, based on the narrower niche/offer identified on Day 2.

**Day 7 — LinkedIn Profile Overhaul**

Step-by-step walkthrough: headline formula (Outcome + Niche + Credibility marker), About section structure (hook → who you help → how → proof → CTA), Featured section (pin 2–3 case studies or results), and recommendations strategy (request 2 from past clients this week).

*Task:* Rewrite your LinkedIn headline and About section using the formulas given. Request at least 2 recommendations from past clients.

**Day 8 — New Service Offer Design**

Lesson covers packaging a service as a productised offer: a named package (e.g. "The 90-Day Growth Sprint") with a fixed scope, fixed price band, and a clear outcome promise. Covers why named packages out-convert "custom quote" offers — they reduce decision fatigue for the buyer and signal expertise.

*Task:* Design your new productised offer: name, scope (3–5 bullet deliverables), price band, and outcome promise.

**Day 9 — Positioning Statement Writing Workshop**

Live/recorded workshop where the student presents their Day 6 positioning drafts and Day 8 offer to the mentor for feedback. Lesson covers common mistakes: vague outcomes ("grow your business"), unverifiable claims, and positioning that doesn't match the proof available from the Day 1 audit.

*Task:* Finalise your positioning statement based on mentor feedback. This is the version used in all outreach going forward.

**Day 10 — Portfolio/Case Study Refresh**

Lesson covers turning the strongest 1–2 results from the Day 1 pipeline audit into a one-page case study (Situation → Action → Result, with numbers wherever possible) that supports the new positioning.

*Task:* Produce 1 refreshed case study aligned to your new niche/offer, in Canva or Notion.

### 📅 Block 2 (Weeks 3–4): Advanced Outreach Systems

**Goal:** Build and run a live, multi-channel outreach campaign at a volume (50+ contacts/day) that experienced freelancers rarely sustain because they lack systems — this block is about systems, not just effort.

**Tools used this block:** [Apollo.io](http://Apollo.io) (advanced sequences), LinkedIn, cold email tooling (Instantly/Smartlead or equivalent), Twitter/X, Slack/Discord communities, CRM (Notion or Bitrix24)

#### Week 3 — Build the System

**Day 11 — Multi-Channel Outreach Strategy & Channel Selection**

Lesson covers why single-channel outreach plateaus (deliverability limits, platform restrictions, audience fatigue) and how to design a 3-channel cadence (e.g. Email → LinkedIn connection → LinkedIn DM, spaced over 5–7 days) tailored to the student's niche from Block 1.

*Task:* Design your 3-channel cadence on paper: which channel for first touch, follow-up 1, follow-up 2, and timing between each.

**Day 12 — Advanced Apollo Sequences (Multi-Step, Conditional)**

Step-by-step: build a 4-step Apollo sequence with conditional branches (if opened but no reply → send variant B; if no open → resend with new subject line). Covers A/B testing subject lines and how to read open/reply rate benchmarks (industry benchmark: 40–60% open rate, 1–5% reply rate for cold email at this stage).

*Task:* Build a live 4-step Apollo sequence with at least one conditional branch, targeting your niche from Block 1.

**Day 13 — LinkedIn Outreach at Scale**

Covers daily connection request limits (notes on platform-imposed caps and how to stay within them safely), InMail vs. connection-first strategy, and message sequencing for LinkedIn (3-touch DM sequence). Reiterates the Sales Navigator caveat from NA-AC-02: covers both the paid Sales Navigator workflow and the free-tier alternative using search + Boolean operators.

*Task:* Send 20 personalised connection requests using your niche-targeted search, with notes referencing something specific about each prospect.

**Day 14 — Cold Email at Scale: Deliverability & Personalisation Tokens**

Lesson covers domain warm-up (why a brand-new sending domain gets flagged as spam, and the 2-week warm-up process), SPF/DKIM/DMARC basics (conceptual, not technical setup — covered at a level a non-technical freelancer can brief a VA on), and personalisation tokens (merge fields) so 50+ emails/day don't read as mass mail.

*Task:* Set up (or verify) your sending domain's warm-up status and draft your email template with at least 3 personalisation tokens.

**Day 15 — Twitter/X & Community Outreach**

Covers finding and engaging in niche-relevant Twitter/X conversations and Slack/Discord communities (where ideal clients discuss their problems publicly), and how to turn engagement into DMs without being spammy — value-first commenting before any pitch.

*Task:* Identify 3 active communities (Slack/Discord/Twitter) relevant to your niche and make 5 genuinely helpful contributions (not pitches) across them.

#### Week 4 — Launch & Run

**Day 16 — Building a Referral Engine from Existing Clients**

Lesson covers the "3-Ask Referral System": (1) ask satisfied past clients for a written testimonial, (2) ask if they know 1–2 people who'd benefit from similar work, (3) offer a finder's fee or reciprocal referral. Covers scripts for each ask.

*Task:* Send referral-request messages to at least 3 past clients using the provided scripts.

**Day 17 — Tracking & Daily Targets Setup**

Covers setting up a CRM dashboard (Notion or Bitrix24) with daily targets per channel (e.g. Email: 30/day, LinkedIn: 15/day, Community: 5 interactions/day = 50 total) and a "contact reached" definition consistent with NA-AC-03's Outreach Sprint SOP (a message delivered and seen — not just sent).

*Task:* Build your CRM dashboard with daily target columns and link your Day 12 Apollo sequence, Day 13 LinkedIn activity, and Day 15 community activity to it.

**Day 18 — Launch Day**

This is a go-live day, not a new lesson — the student launches all three channels simultaneously and logs results in real time. Mentor is available for troubleshooting (deliverability issues, sequence errors, etc.).

*Task:* Launch your full multi-channel campaign. Log every send/contact in your CRM dashboard.

**Day 19 — Reply Handling & Triage**

Lesson covers the 3 reply types (Interested, Not Now, Not Interested/Negative) and a response script for each, plus how to fast-track "Interested" replies into a discovery call booking within 24 hours — speed-to-lead is the #1 differentiator at this volume.

*Task:* Respond to every reply received so far using the appropriate script. Book at least 1 discovery call if any "Interested" replies were received.

**Day 20 — Mid-Campaign Optimisation & Reporting**

Lesson covers reading week 1 campaign data (open rates, reply rates, connection acceptance rates) and making one optimisation per channel (new subject line, new LinkedIn message angle, etc.) for week 2 of the live campaign, which continues through Block 3.

*Task:* Submit a 1-page Campaign Report: total contacts reached per channel, response rate, and one optimisation you're making going into Block 3.

### 📅 Block 3 (Weeks 5–6): Closing, Pricing & Retainers

**Goal:** Move from one-off projects to recurring retainer revenue at premium ($1,500–$5,000/month) rates — the core income jump this track is designed to produce.

**Tools used this block:** Proposal tools (Notion/Google Docs/PandaDoc), Loom (for proposal walkthroughs), CRM

#### Week 5 — Pricing & Proposals

**Day 21 — Premium Pricing Psychology**

Lesson covers why clients paying $1,500–$5,000/month behave differently from $100 gig-buyers: they care about reliability, communication, and outcomes more than price itself, and they're often more loyal once committed. Covers value-based pricing (anchored to the client's revenue/cost impact, not the freelancer's time) vs. hourly pricing.

*Task:* For your productised offer (from Block 1, Day 8), calculate a value-based price by estimating the client's potential revenue/cost impact and pricing at 10–20% of that estimated impact (within the $1,500–$5,000/month band).

**Day 22 — Retainer Offer Structure**

Lesson covers what makes a retainer "can't say no" — a clear monthly scope, a defined cadence of deliverables (e.g. weekly reports, monthly strategy call), and a 90-day minimum commitment framed as needed for results, not as a lock-in trap. Covers tiered retainer structures (Starter/Growth/Scale) so clients can self-select.

*Task:* Build a 3-tier retainer offer (Starter/Growth/Scale) with scope and price for each tier.

**Day 23 — Discovery Call for High-Ticket Clients**

Builds on the SPIN framework from ISP-05, adapted for retainer conversations: extra emphasis on the "Implication" and "Need-Payoff" questions to help the prospect articulate the cost of inaction (important for justifying premium pricing). Includes a role-play scenario with the mentor.

*Task:* Role-play a high-ticket discovery call with your mentor using the adapted SPIN framework. Record and review together.

**Day 24 — Proposal Writing for Retainers**

Lesson covers proposal structure for retainers: Situation Summary → Recommended Tier → Scope & Deliverables → Investment → Timeline → Next Steps. Covers common mistakes: too much jargon, no clear next step, and pricing buried at the bottom instead of framed around value delivered.

*Task:* Write a full retainer proposal for one warm prospect using the structure given.

**Day 25 — Sending Proposals & Follow-Up Cadence**

Covers how to send a proposal (live walkthrough call vs. async with a Loom video) and a 3-touch follow-up cadence (Day 2, Day 5, Day 9 after sending) with scripts for each touch that add value rather than just "checking in."

*Task:* Send your Day 24 proposal to at least 1 warm prospect, with a Loom walkthrough video. Identify 4 more warm prospects to send proposals to this week.

#### Week 6 — Negotiation & Closing

**Day 26 — Advanced Objection Handling for High-Ticket Clients**

Lesson covers the top 5 high-ticket objections ("too expensive," "need to think about it," "need to check with my partner/boss," "can we start smaller," "can you guarantee results") with a response framework for each: acknowledge → reframe → evidence → next step.

*Task:* Write your personalised response script for each of the 5 objections, referencing your own case studies as evidence.

**Day 27 — Negotiation Tactics: Anchoring & Concessions**

Covers anchoring (presenting the higher tier first so the middle tier looks reasonable), and how to make concessions strategically (e.g. trading a lower price for a longer commitment or a case study/testimonial rights) rather than just discounting.

*Task:* For your active proposal conversations, identify one non-price concession you could offer instead of a discount if asked.

**Day 28 — Contracts & Onboarding for Retainer Clients**

Lesson covers what a retainer contract must include (scope, payment terms, cancellation/notice period, IP ownership, confidentiality) and a basic client onboarding checklist (kickoff call agenda, access/credentials checklist, communication channel setup — referencing NA-AC-08's Slack standards).

*Task:* Prepare a retainer contract template (can adapt a standard template) and an onboarding checklist for your first retainer client.

**Day 29 — Handling "Think About It" & Stalled Deals**

Covers the psychology of stalled deals (usually unaddressed objections or lack of urgency, not actual disinterest) and a re-engagement sequence: a value-add follow-up (share a relevant resource/insight) followed by a direct "is this still a priority?" message after 7 days of silence.

*Task:* For any proposals sent that have gone quiet, send a value-add follow-up using the framework.

**Day 30 — Closing Week Review & Pipeline Update**

Review week: update the CRM dashboard with the status of every proposal sent this block, and identify which deals are likely to close in the next 2 weeks (carried into Block 4).

*Task:* Submit an updated CRM pipeline view showing all 5 proposals, their stage, and next action for each.
