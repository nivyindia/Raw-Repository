## Module 5 — Negotiation & Closing (Week 9–10)

### Week 9: The Art of the Sales Call

**Week 9 Learning Goal:** By Friday, you have practised the SPIN discovery call framework, can handle the 4 most common objections, and have recorded a simulated discovery call with your mentor.

---

**Monday — Lesson 9.1: Discovery Call Structure**

The discovery call is the most important conversation in the sales process. It determines whether a client buys, what they buy, and at what price.

**The 4-stage structure:**

**Stage 1 — Opening (2 minutes):**

"Thanks for making time, [Name]. I've set aside 30 minutes — does that still work? Great. I'd love to start by learning more about your business and then I can share how I typically help. Does that work for you?"

**Stage 2 — Discovery / SPIN (15 minutes):**

Run through your SPIN questions (from Month 2 Lesson 6.4). Let the client talk. The person asking questions controls the conversation — ask, then listen. Do not pitch yet.

**Stage 3 — Solution Presentation (8 minutes):**

"Based on what you've described, here's how I'd approach it: [specific service] → [specific deliverable] → [specific result]. This is what I typically do for clients in your situation."

**Stage 4 — Next Steps (5 minutes):**

"Does that sound like what you're looking for?" → If yes: "Great. I'll send you a proposal within 48 hours. What's the best email for that?" → If questions: answer them → If not a fit: refer or close gracefully.

**Tuesday — Lesson 9.2: SPIN Selling for Digital Marketing Services**

SPIN is a framework for uncovering a client's real problem and helping them realise the cost of inaction.

**Full SPIN sequence for your niche (example: lead generation for SaaS):**

- **Situation:** "How are you currently generating new leads for your SaaS?"
- **Problem:** "What's been the biggest challenge with that approach?"
- **Implication:** "How long has that been the case? How much is that limiting your monthly revenue growth?"
- **Need-payoff:** "If you had a consistent system bringing in 20–30 qualified leads per month, what would that change for your team?"

After the need-payoff question, the client tells you their own reason to buy. You are not selling — you are helping them articulate their problem clearly enough that the solution is obvious.

**SPIN rules:**

- Ask one question at a time. Never stack: "What's your challenge and how long has it been happening and what have you tried?"
- Listen more than you talk (70/30 rule: client talks 70%, you talk 30%)
- Take brief notes — it signals you are listening and helps with the proposal

**Wednesday — Lesson 9.3: Handling the 4 Most Common Objections**

An objection is not a "no" — it is a request for more information or reassurance.

**Objection 1: "Your price is too high."**

Response: "I understand — can I ask what budget you have in mind? [Listen.] I see. Let me tell you what's included at my price and you can decide if the value makes sense. [Explain deliverables.] Alternatively, I could start with a smaller scope at [lower price] to show you the results first."

**Objection 2: "Let me think about it."**

Response: "Of course — what specifically would help you decide? Is it the price, the scope, or something else?" [Listen and address that specific concern.] "Would it help if I sent you a proposal so you have everything in writing to review?"

**Objection 3: "I've tried this before and it didn't work."**

Response: "I'm sorry to hear that — what happened? [Listen fully.] That's really useful context. What I'd do differently is [specific difference in your approach]. Would you be open to a 30-day trial so you can see the results before committing to a longer engagement?"

**Objection 4: "I'll do it myself."**

Response: "That's totally valid. Can I ask — how much time do you have to spend on [specific service] each week? [Listen.] The reason most businesses eventually outsource this is that it frees up [X hours/week] that can go toward [what they care about]. But you know your situation best."

**Week 10 Learning Goal:** By Friday, you have written a complete mock proposal, understand how to collect payment, and have built a client onboarding system.

**Monday — Lesson 10.1: Proposal Structure**

A proposal is a document sent after a positive discovery call. It converts interest into a signed agreement.

**The 6-section proposal structure:**

1. **Problem:** Summarise what the client told you in the discovery call. Show you listened. "Based on our conversation, [Company] is currently [situation]. The main challenge is [problem] which is resulting in [implication]."
2. **Solution:** Describe your proposed approach. Be specific. "I propose a 3-month lead generation retainer covering: [specific deliverables]. Here is how I'll approach Month 1..."
3. **Deliverables:** List every item the client receives. Be explicit. "Monthly deliverables: 1 prospect list (50 verified leads) / 1 Apollo email sequence (3 steps) / Weekly report / 1 strategy call per month."
4. **Timeline:** "Month 1: Setup and first campaign. Month 2: Optimise and scale. Month 3: Full retainer run with performance review."
5. **Pricing:** 3-tier pricing is most effective for closing:
    - Starter: $[X]/month — [fewer deliverables]
    - Growth: $[Y]/month — [full deliverables] — **"Recommended"**
    - Premium: $[Z]/month — [full deliverables + extras]
6. **CTA:** "If you'd like to move forward with the Growth package, simply reply to this email and I'll send a contract and invoice within 24 hours."

**Tuesday — Lesson 10.2: Contract Basics**

You must have a signed contract before starting any paid work. No exceptions.

**What every freelance contract must include:**

- Names and contact details of both parties
- Scope of work (exactly what is included and what is NOT included)
- Deliverables and deadlines
- Payment terms (how much, when, how)
- Revision policy (how many rounds, what counts as a revision)
- Termination clause (how either party can end the contract and with how much notice)
- Intellectual property clause (client owns the work once paid)
- Non-disclosure (you won't share their business information)

**Where to get contracts:**

- Free template: Bonsai ([bonsai.io](http://bonsai.io)) → "Contract Templates" → "Freelance Marketing Contract"
- Customise it with your details and scope — do not send the template as-is
- Use a tool like Bonsai, HelloSign, or DocuSign for digital signature (free tiers available)

**Wednesday — Lesson 10.3: Payment Collection**

You must collect payment before starting work (or on a 50% upfront, 50% on completion model for project work).

**Payment platforms for international clients:**

- **Wise (formerly TransferWise):** Best for receiving USD/GBP/EUR to INR. Low fees. Fast. Recommended for international clients.
- **PayPal:** Widely used in USA/Australia. Higher fees (3–4%). Easy to set up.

**How to send an invoice:**

1. Create a free account on Invoice Ninja, Wave, or Bonsai

3. Add line items: "Digital Marketing Retainer — Month 1 — [Service]" → Amount in USD
4. Set payment terms: "Due within 7 days of invoice date"
5. Add your Wise or PayPal email as the payment destination

**Week 11 Learning Goal:** By Friday, you have built a personal Client Delivery SOP, understand how to use AI to accelerate delivery, and have a draft retainer model.

**Monday — Lesson 11.1: Building a Repeatable Delivery System**

The biggest mistake freelancers make is treating every client as a unique project from scratch. The highest-earning freelancers build systems they run for every client.

**A delivery system means:**

- Every client goes through the same onboarding process (same form, same checklist, same welcome email structure)
- Every service has a documented process (SOP) so it can be done consistently
- Every week has a fixed structure: Monday plan / Tuesday–Thursday delivery / Friday report and review
- Every month ends with a structured report and review call

**Why this matters:**

- Consistency means fewer errors and missed deadlines
- Systems can be delegated (if you eventually hire a sub-VA)
- Systems mean you can manage 3–5 clients without burning out

**Daily Task (Monday):** Map out a "week in the life" of managing one client. What happens on Monday? Tuesday? Wednesday? Thursday? Friday? Write it as a day-by-day schedule. This becomes the backbone of your delivery SOP.

---

**Tuesday — Lesson 11.2: Using AI to 3x Your Output**

AI does not replace your thinking — it accelerates your execution.

**High-ROI AI use cases in client delivery:**

- **Content creation:** Brief AI → get 5 post drafts → edit 2 → publish. Time saved: 2 hours per week
- **Email copywriting:** Feed AI the client's product + target audience → get 5 subject lines + 3 email drafts → edit and personalise → send. Time saved: 1–2 hours per campaign
- **Research:** "Summarise the top 3 competitors for [client company] in [market]" → AI gives you a starting point to refine
- **Reporting:** Paste your raw metrics into Claude → "Write a plain-English summary of these results for a non-technical client"
- **Proposal drafts:** Feed AI the client brief + your service → get a proposal first draft → edit and personalise

**What AI cannot do:**

- Build genuine relationships with clients
- Make strategic decisions based on business context
- Replace real outreach personalisation
- Produce accurate data or verified facts (always verify numbers AI gives you)

**Wednesday — Lesson 11.3: Managing Multiple Clients Without Burning Out**

Once you have 2–3 clients, workload management becomes critical.

**The 3-client management system:**

- Each client gets a dedicated Notion workspace (from Week 10)
- You work on clients in blocks, not simultaneously: "Monday morning = Client A / Monday afternoon = Client B"
- Weekly check-in message sent to every client on Friday (2–3 sentences updating them even if there's nothing dramatic to report)
- Never take on a new client unless you have documented how you will deliver for them — no "I'll figure it out"

**Red flags that you are overcommitted:**

- You are missing deadlines or sending apology emails
- Your Daily Log shows fewer than 5 tasks completed most days
- You are not updating your CRM or Notion workspace
- You feel anxious every Monday morning about client work

If you hit red flags: notify your mentor immediately. Do not accept new clients. Stabilise existing relationships first.

**Thursday — Lesson 11.4: Monthly Retainers — The Path to Recurring Revenue**

A retainer is a monthly agreement where a client pays you a fixed fee for an ongoing set of deliverables.

**Why retainers are better than one-off projects:**

- Predictable income — you know your revenue for the next 3 months
- Deeper client relationships — you understand their business over time
- Less selling — you are not constantly looking for new clients
- Higher lifetime value per client

**How to convert a project client to a retainer:**

After completing the first project or first month: "The results so far are [specific result]. To build on this consistently, I'd recommend moving to a monthly retainer. Here's what that would include: [deliverables]. The investment is $[price]/month."

**Retainer pricing formula:**

- Estimate total hours per month for that client: e.g., 20 hours
- Multiply by your target hourly rate: 20 × $25 = $500/month minimum
- Add a management premium: +20% for coordination, reporting, calls = $600/month
- Price it at the nearest round number that feels right: $600/month
