# P19.1.1 — AI Basics: What Generative AI Actually Is

**Learning Objectives:** By the end, students can explain (in plain language) what an LLM is, how it differs from a search engine, and why this matters for marketing work.

**Outline:**

1. Hook — "You've used AI today without realising it" (autocomplete, recommendations, spam filters)
2. What is Generative AI vs. Traditional Software (rule-based vs. pattern-based)
3. How LLMs work — the "next word prediction" explanation, no jargon
4. AI vs. Search Engines — key differences students will feel daily (no single "right" answer, hallucination risk, context windows)
5. Why this matters for a marketer — frame AI as a *force multiplier* for content, research, and analysis, not a replacement for strategy
6. Common myths debunked ("AI will take my job" vs. "AI changes what my job looks like")
7. Checkpoint quiz (ties to Sub-Topic Checkpoint template from QW-05)

**Recording notes:** Use Faculty Recording & Slide Standards SOP (QW-10). Keep this conversational — this is the most "non-technical learner" facing module in the whole catalog.

# P19.1.2 — Prompt Engineering Fundamentals for Marketers

**Learning Objectives:** Students can write a structured prompt using role, context, task, format, and constraints — and iterate on results.

**Outline:**

1. Hook — side-by-side: vague prompt vs. structured prompt, same tool, very different outputs
2. The RTCF framework: Role, Task, Context, Format (introduce as a repeatable template)
3. Worked example #1 — Writing a social media caption (vague → structured → refined)
4. Worked example #2 — Summarising a competitor's blog post for content ideas
5. Iteration techniques — follow-up prompts, asking AI to critique its own output, providing examples (few-shot)
6. Common mistakes — over-stuffing prompts, not specifying audience/tone, trusting first output blindly
7. Practical exercise (ties to Module Practical Exam template from QW-05) — students write 3 prompts for 3 different marketing tasks
8. Checkpoint quiz

**Recording notes:** This is the highest-leverage module in the set — consider this the "hero" sub-module for marketing in promo material.

# P19.1.3 — AI Tools Landscape for Digital Marketing

**Learning Objectives:** Students can name and categorise the major AI tool categories relevant to DM and identify which tool fits which task.

**Outline:**

1. Hook — "There are hundreds of AI tools — here's how to never feel overwhelmed again"
2. Categorisation framework: (a) General-purpose chat assistants, (b) Content generation (text/image/video), (c) SEO & research AI tools, (d) Automation/agentic tools, (e) Analytics & data AI
3. Walkthrough of 1–2 representative tools per category (keep tool-agnostic where possible — avoid hard dependency on any single paid tool given rapid market change)
4. "Build vs. buy" mindset — free tiers vs. paid, when upgrading makes sense for a freelancer/agency
5. Quarterly AI Tools Audit teaser — connect forward to P19.4 (Quarterly AI tools audit protocol, Wave 1 Oct)
6. Hands-on activity — students pick one tool from each category and complete a micro-task
7. Checkpoint quiz

**Recording notes:** Flag to AI Faculty Lead — this module has the shortest shelf life of the four and is the strongest candidate for the P19.4 quarterly refresh cycle once that protocol is live.

# P19.1.4 — AI Ethics, Limitations & Responsible Use

**Learning Objectives:** Students can identify hallucination risk, bias, copyright/IP concerns, and data privacy issues when using AI tools professionally.

**Outline:**

1. Hook — real (anonymised) example of an AI-generated factual error in a marketing context and its consequences
2. Hallucinations — why they happen, how to fact-check AI output before publishing
3. Bias in AI outputs — how training data shapes results, implications for inclusive marketing
4. Copyright & IP — using AI-generated content commercially, attribution norms, current legal landscape (high-level, not legal advice)
5. Data privacy — what NOT to paste into AI tools (client data, PII) — directly previews Phase 14 (DPDP Act / data privacy modules, Wave 6)
6. Building an "AI disclosure" habit — when and how to disclose AI use to clients/employers
7. Responsible use checklist (downloadable resource)
8. Checkpoint quiz + reflection prompt

**Recording notes:** This module is a natural cross-link to Phase 14 (Legal & Compliance, Wave 6) — Content Team should be looped in so messaging is consistent when Phase 14 modules are built ~18 months later.
