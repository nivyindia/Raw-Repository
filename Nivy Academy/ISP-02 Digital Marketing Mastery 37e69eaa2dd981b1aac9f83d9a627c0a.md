# Week 5 — SEO (Search Engine Optimisation)

## Lesson 5.1 — How Search Engines Work (International SEO Context)

**Lesson Content:**

Search engines are not magic — they are systems that follow rules. Understanding those rules is what separates a digital marketer from someone who just posts content and hopes for the best.

Google's job is to find the best possible answer for a user's search query and deliver it on the results page. To do this, Google sends automated programs called **crawlers** (also called spiders or bots) that browse the web, following links from page to page. As they visit each page, they read the content and send that information back to Google's servers. This process is called **crawling**.

Once a page has been crawled, Google decides whether to include it in its search index — the enormous catalogue of all web pages it knows about. This is called **indexing**. Not all pages get indexed. If your page is blocked by a robots.txt file, has no inbound links, or has thin/duplicate content, Google may skip it.

Finally, when a user types a query, Google **ranks** all indexed pages by relevance and quality, displaying results in order. Where your page appears in those results is your ranking.

**E-E-A-T: The Quality Standard Google Uses**

Since 2022, Google evaluates content by four criteria:

- **Experience** — Does the author have real first-hand experience with the topic?
- **Expertise** — Do they have relevant knowledge or credentials?
- **Authoritativeness** — Does the wider web recognize them as credible (links, mentions)?
- **Trustworthiness** — Is the site secure, transparent, and accurate?

For international clients, this matters because US and UK users (and Google's algorithms for those markets) apply a higher quality bar than many Indian domestic websites are used to meeting. You need to produce content that reads like a subject matter expert wrote it — not keyword-stuffed filler.

**Why International SEO Differs**

When working with a US or UK client, you are competing in a far more crowded SEO landscape. Average domain authority of top-ranking pages is much higher. Keyword volumes are 5–20x larger but so is competition. Local SEO factors differ (Google My Business in the UK uses a different process than India). You must also be aware of Google Search Console settings for target country if the site serves one market.

**Tools You Will Use:**

- **Google Search Console** — free; shows how your site appears in Google search, which queries trigger it, click-through rates, and technical errors
- **SEMrush** — paid (free trial available); best for keyword research, competitor analysis, and site audits
- **Ahrefs** — paid (limited free features); best for backlink analysis
- **PageSpeed Insights** — free; checks site speed and Core Web Vitals

## Lesson 5.2 — On-Page SEO: Content & Structure

**Lesson Content:**

On-page SEO refers to everything on your actual web page that signals to Google what the page is about and whether it deserves to rank.

**Title Tags**

The title tag is the blue clickable text you see in Google search results. It is also shown in the browser tab. Rules:

- Include the target keyword, ideally at the start
- Keep it under 60 characters (Google cuts off longer titles)
- Make it specific and compelling — it competes with 9 other results
- Bad: "Home | XYZ Digital" Good: "Digital Marketing Agency for US Startups | XYZ Digital"

**Meta Descriptions**

The grey text shown below the title in search results. It does not directly affect ranking, but it affects click-through rate — which does. Rules:

- Under 155 characters
- Include the keyword naturally
- End with a clear CTA: "Get your free audit today" or "See how we helped 50+ US clients"
- Each page needs a unique meta description — never duplicate

**H1/H2/H3 Structure**

Headings tell both readers and Google how your content is organised. Every page should have exactly one H1 (the main topic), multiple H2s (main sections), and H3s for sub-points within sections. Include your primary keyword in the H1 and related keywords in H2s naturally.

**Internal Linking**

Linking from one page on your site to another passes authority around your site and helps Google understand which pages are most important. Best practice: every new blog post should link to at least 3 existing pages on the site, and at least one existing page should link back to it.

**Image Alt Text**

Google cannot see images — it reads the alt text to understand what an image shows. Write descriptive alt text for every image: not "image1.jpg" but "screenshot of SEMrush keyword overview for 'digital marketing agency'"

**Content Length Benchmarks for International Markets:**

- Blog posts for US/UK audiences: 1,200–2,500 words for most topics; 3,000+ for pillar pages
- Service pages: 600–1,200 words
- Product pages: 300–600 words with strong specifications
- Short content (under 300 words) rarely ranks for competitive keywords

## Lesson 5.3 — Off-Page SEO: Link Building for International Clients

**Lesson Content:**

If on-page SEO is about relevance, off-page SEO is about authority. Google treats a backlink (another website linking to yours) as a vote of confidence. The more authoritative and relevant the site linking to you, the more that link is worth.

**Why Backlinks Are Critical for International Clients**

For competitive US and UK markets, on-page SEO alone is rarely enough. If your client's competitor has 500 high-authority backlinks and your client has 20, the competitor will outrank you for almost every keyword, even if your content is better. Link building is the hard, slow work that separates strong SEO strategies from weak ones.

**White-Hat Link Building Strategies:**

1. **Guest Posting** — Write a useful article for another website in exchange for a link back to your client's site. Target publications with Domain Authority (DA) 40+. Pitch with a personalised email, specific article idea, and 2–3 writing samples.
2. **HARO (Help A Reporter Out)** — Journalists and bloggers post requests for expert quotes. Sign up at [helpareporter.com](http://helpareporter.com), monitor requests in your niche, and respond with a concise, expert-quality answer. If used, you get a backlink from their publication — often high-DA news sites.
3. **Digital PR** — Create data-driven content (surveys, studies, original research) that journalists will naturally want to cite. Example: "We surveyed 500 remote workers about their productivity tools" → pitch to HR, tech, and business publications.
4. **Broken Link Building** — Use Ahrefs to find pages on high-DA sites that link to dead URLs. Create content that replaces the dead content, then email the site owner suggesting they update the link to yours.

**What You Should NEVER Do:**

- Buy links (Google penalises this)
- Use private blog networks (PBNs)
- Exchange links en masse ("link for link" schemes)
- Submit to low-quality directories

**Tools:**

- **Ahrefs** — enter any competitor's domain, click "Backlinks" to see where their links come from. Sort by DR (Domain Rating) to find the highest-value sources.
- **BuzzSumo** — find the most shared content in any niche. Use it to identify publications and journalists who write about your client's topics.

## Lesson 5.4 — Technical SEO & Site Speed

**Lesson Content:**

Technical SEO deals with the infrastructure of a website — making sure search engines can access it, read it, and evaluate it correctly. Even perfect content can be invisible to Google if the technical foundations are broken.

**Core Web Vitals — Google's Performance Standards**

Google officially uses Core Web Vitals as a ranking factor. These measure the user experience of your page:

- **LCP (Largest Contentful Paint):** How long does the main content take to load? Target: under 2.5 seconds. Poor: over 4 seconds.
- **FID (First Input Delay) / now INP (Interaction to Next Paint):** How long before the page responds to a user click? Target: under 200ms.
- **CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift):** Does the page jump around as it loads (buttons moving, text shifting)? Target: under 0.1.

Check all three at [**pagespeed.web.dev**](http://pagespeed.web.dev) — Google's free tool.

**XML Sitemaps**

A sitemap is a file that lists every page on your site and tells Google where to find them. Most CMSs (WordPress, Wix, Shopify) generate these automatically. Submit the sitemap URL in Google Search Console: Settings → Sitemaps.

**Robots.txt**

A text file that tells crawlers which pages NOT to crawl. Mistakes here can accidentally block your whole site from Google. Check by visiting [yourdomain.com/robots.txt](http://yourdomain.com/robots.txt) — make sure important pages are not disallowed.

**HTTPS**

All modern websites must use HTTPS (the padlock in the browser). HTTP sites are flagged as "Not Secure" by Chrome and are penalised by Google. If a client's site is still on HTTP, this is a Priority 1 fix.

**Mobile-First Indexing**

Google now indexes the mobile version of your site first. If the mobile version is missing content that the desktop version has, you lose those rankings. Test mobile usability in Google Search Console under "Mobile Usability" or at [search.google.com/test/mobile-friendly](http://search.google.com/test/mobile-friendly).

**Schema Markup**

Schema is code you add to a page that helps Google understand what the content is (a product, a review, a FAQ, an event). It can generate rich results in search (star ratings, FAQs expanding directly in Google). Most useful schema types for client sites: FAQ, Review, Product, LocalBusiness, Article.

# Week 6 — Google Ads & Paid Search (PPC)

## Lesson 6.1 — PPC Fundamentals for International Markets

**Lesson Content:**

PPC (Pay-Per-Click) means you pay only when someone clicks your ad. Google Ads is the largest PPC platform in the world, and it is the skill most US and UK marketing agencies will test you on in an interview or on a freelance brief.

**How the Google Ads Auction Works**

Every time someone types a search query, Google runs an instant auction among advertisers bidding on that keyword. The winner is NOT simply the highest bidder — Google uses **Ad Rank** to decide placement:

> **Ad Rank = Bid × Quality Score × Expected Impact of Extensions**
> 

This means a well-optimised ad with a lower bid can outrank a poor ad with a higher bid.

**Quality Score (1–10)**

Google scores your ad on three things:

1. **Expected Click-Through Rate** — how likely is this ad to be clicked based on historical data?
2. **Ad Relevance** — how closely does your ad match the user’s search query?
3. **Landing Page Experience** — is the page the user lands on relevant, fast, and useful?

A Quality Score of 7–10 means your ads cost less per click than competitors with lower scores. This is why great copywriting and landing page quality save clients real money.

**Campaign Structure:**

- **Campaign** (sets the budget, targeting geography, bidding strategy)
    - **Ad Group** (a theme or topic — e.g., "SEO Services", "PPC Services")
        - **Ads** (the actual text the user sees)
        - **Keywords** (the search terms that trigger this ad group)

Always match keyword themes tightly to ad group themes. Never throw 50 unrelated keywords into one ad group.

**Keyword Match Types:**

- **Broad Match** — shows for loosely related queries. High reach, low control. Use carefully.
- **Phrase Match** — shows when the search includes your phrase. Good balance of reach and control.
- **Exact Match** — shows only for that specific query (or very close variants). High control, lower volume.

**Bidding Strategies (Simplified):**

- **Manual CPC** — you set each bid. Best for new accounts with no data.
- **Target CPA (Cost Per Acquisition)** — Google auto-bids to get conversions at your target cost. Needs 30+ conversions per month to work well.
- **Target ROAS** — Google bids to achieve your return on ad spend target. E-commerce focused.
- **Maximise Conversions** — Google spends your budget to get the most conversions. Good early-stage option.

**Managing USD Budgets**

When clients pay in USD, every dollar of wasted spend is visible. Even a 10% improvement in CPC through better Quality Score can save $500–$2,000/month on a $5,000/month budget. Always frame your work in terms of cost saved and revenue generated — not just metrics.

## Lesson 6.2 — Search Campaigns: From Setup to Launch

**Lesson Content:**

Building a Google Search campaign from scratch is one of the most requested skills for digital marketing freelancers. Here is the exact process:

**Step 1 — Account Structure**

Before touching Google Ads, map your campaign structure on paper:

- How many campaigns? (Usually one per product/service line or target geography)
- How many ad groups per campaign? (Usually 3–8, each with a tight keyword theme)
- How many ads per ad group? (Google recommends 3–5 Responsive Search Ads per group)

**Step 2 — Writing Headlines + Descriptions**

Google Responsive Search Ads (RSAs) let you input up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions. Google mixes and matches them automatically to find the best combinations.

Headlines (30 characters max each):

- At least 3 should include the exact keyword
- At least 3 should include a value proposition ("Free Quote", "UK's #1 Rated", "50+ 5-Star Reviews")
- At least 3 should include a CTA ("Book a Free Call", "Get Instant Access", "Start Today")

Descriptions (90 characters max each):

- Expand on the headline’s promise
- Include one specific benefit (not just what you do, but what the client gets)
- One description should address an objection ("No long-term contracts", "Cancel anytime")

**Step 3 — Ad Extensions (now called "Assets")**

Extensions make your ad take up more space and provide more information:

- **Sitelinks** — additional links below your ad ("Our Services", "Pricing", "Client Reviews")
- **Callouts** — short text snippets ("Free Audit", "Award-Winning", "Since 2015")
- **Structured Snippets** — lists of services, products, or locations
- **Call Extension** — adds your phone number to mobile ads
- **Lead Form Extension** — users fill out a form without leaving Google

Always add extensions — they improve Quality Score and increase ad real estate.

**Step 4 — Negative Keywords**

Negative keywords prevent your ad from showing on irrelevant searches. Example: if you run ads for a premium law firm, add "cheap", "free", "DIY" as negatives so you don’t waste budget on people who won’t pay your rates. Review the Search Terms report weekly and add new negatives regularly.

**Step 5 — Campaign Settings to Check Before Launch:**

- Search network only (uncheck Display network)
- Correct geographic targeting (city, region, or country)
- Language targeting (English)
- Start date + daily budget confirmed
- Conversion tracking verified

## Lesson 6.3 — Performance Max, Display & Remarketing

**Lesson Content:**

**Performance Max (PMax)**

Performance Max is Google’s AI-driven campaign type that runs across all Google channels simultaneously — Search, Display, YouTube, Gmail, Maps, and Discover. You give Google your creative assets (headlines, descriptions, images, videos) and it decides where and when to show them.

When to use PMax:

- When the client already has conversion data (30+ conversions in the last 30 days)
- E-commerce clients with Google Merchant Center product feeds
- Clients who want maximum reach with minimum manual management

When NOT to use PMax:

- New accounts with no conversion history (Google won’t have data to optimise)
- Very tight budget (under $1,000/month) — PMax needs budget to learn
- Clients who need full control of where ads show

**Google Display Network**

Display ads are banner/image ads shown on websites, apps, and YouTube. They are for awareness and remarketing — not for direct response. Do not use Display as a lead generation tool without warm audiences.

Targeting options:

- **Affinity audiences** — people with long-term interests (e.g., "Small Business Owners")
- **In-market audiences** — people actively researching a purchase (e.g., "CRM software")
- **Custom audiences** — based on keywords they’ve searched or sites they’ve visited
- **Placement targeting** — specific websites or YouTube channels

**Remarketing — The Most Efficient PPC Spend**

Remarketing shows ads to people who have already visited your website. These users convert at 2–5x the rate of cold audiences because they already know who you are. To run remarketing:

1. Install the Google Ads Tag (or GA4 linked to Google Ads) on the client’s site
2. Create audience lists: "All website visitors last 30 days", "Cart abandoners", "Pricing page visitors"
3. Create a Display or Search Remarketing campaign targeting those lists
4. Use different ad copy for warm audiences — they already know you, so skip the introduction

**Creative Requirements for Display Ads:**

- Responsive Display Ads: provide up to 15 images, 5 logos, 5 short headlines, 1 long headline, 5 descriptions
- Image sizes that must be provided: 1200×628 (landscape), 1200×1200 (square), 300×600 (half page)
- Always include the brand logo separately from the image
- Use Canva to create display ad sets — it has a Google Ads template library

## Lesson 6.4 — Reading & Reporting Google Ads Results

**Lesson Content:**

The ability to read campaign data and tell a clear story to a client is what separates a junior PPC manager from a trusted advisor. Most clients don’t understand CTR or CPC — they understand whether they made money.

**The Metrics That Matter — and What They Mean:**

| Metric | What It Measures | Good Benchmark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CTR (Click-Through Rate) | % of people who saw the ad and clicked | Search: 3–5%+ · Display: 0.1–0.5% |
| CPC (Cost Per Click) | Average cost of each click | Varies by industry: legal $5–50, SaaS $3–15 |
| CPA (Cost Per Acquisition) | Cost to get one conversion (lead or sale) | Should be below client’s profit margin |
| ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) | Revenue ÷ Ad Spend | E-commerce target: 3–5x minimum |
| Conversion Rate | % of clicks that converted | 2–5% is typical for well-optimised landing pages |
| Quality Score | Google’s 1–10 rating of your ad relevance | Target 7–10 |
| Impression Share | % of available auctions where your ad showed | Low impression share = bid or budget too low |

**How to Build a Weekly Google Ads Report for a Client:**

1. **Executive Summary** (3 sentences): What happened this week? Up or down vs last week? Why?
2. **Spend Summary**: Total spend, clicks, impressions, CTR — vs last week and last month
3. **Conversion Summary**: Total leads/sales, CPA, ROAS — are we hitting targets?
4. **Top Performing Ads**: Which ads had the highest CTR and conversion rate? Screenshot them.
5. **Top Performing Keywords**: Which keywords drove the most conversions?
6. **Issues Found**: Any keywords with high spend but zero conversions? Any ads with CTR under 1%?
7. **Next Week’s Actions**: 3 specific things you will change or test and why

**What to Say When Results Are Bad:**

Clients panic when they see numbers going down. Your job is to:

- Be honest: don’t hide poor performance
- Explain the cause: was it seasonal? A landing page issue? A competitor increase?
- Show what you’re doing about it: specific, actionable next steps
- Set expectations: how long before they should see improvement?

Never send a report with bad numbers and no explanation. That’s how you lose clients.

# Week 7 — Social Media Marketing (Organic + Paid)

## Lesson 7.1 — Platform Strategy for International Clients

**Duration:** 60 min

**Lesson Content:**

Not every platform is right for every client. One of the most common mistakes junior marketers make is spreading a client's budget and energy across every social platform instead of dominating the one or two that actually contain their audience. Here is how to think about this correctly.

**Platform-by-Market Decision Framework:**

| Platform | Best Market Fit | Content Format | Posting Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LinkedIn | B2B, US/UK, corporate, SaaS, consulting | Long-form posts, carousels, thought leadership | 3–5x per week |
| Instagram | DTC (direct-to-consumer), fashion, beauty, food, lifestyle | Reels (priority), carousels, Stories | 5–7x per week |
| Facebook | 35+ audience, local services, US/Australia, community groups | Video, images, groups, events | 3–5x per week |
| TikTok | Gen Z (18–24), consumer brands, edgy/fun brands, viral potential | Short-form video only | Daily (1–3x) |
| Twitter/X | Thought leadership, SaaS, tech, crypto, media companies | Text threads, opinions, news commentary | 5–10x per week |
| YouTube | Education, tutorials, product demos, long-form content | Long-form video (10–20 min), Shorts | 1–2x per week |
| Pinterest | US women 25–45, home, food, DIY, fashion, travel | Vertical images, infographics | 10–20 pins/week |

**The 60/20/20 Content Mix Rule:**

For any platform, structure your content calendar as:

- **60% Educational/Valuable** — teach your audience something, share insights, how-tos, industry news
- **20% Entertainment/Relatable** — behind-the-scenes, memes (brand-appropriate), stories, personality
- **20% Promotional** — offers, services, case studies, calls to action

Violating this ratio (too much promotion) causes follower drop-off and reduced organic reach. Clients often push back on this — your job is to educate them.

**Organic vs Paid Social — When to Use Each:**

- **Organic** — builds long-term brand trust, community, and inbound leads. Slow but compound returns. Use for content marketing and thought leadership.
- **Paid** — immediate reach, scalable, precise targeting. Requires budget. Use for lead generation, product launches, and retargeting.

Most clients need both. Organic warms the audience; paid captures demand.

**Canva for Social Media — Sizes and Setup:**

- 4 LinkedIn text posts (one insight per post)
- 4 Instagram carousel slides (key points visualised)
- 2 Reels or TikToks (short 30–60 second takeaways)
- 1 email newsletter
- 5 Twitter/X tweets (individual quotes or stats)

This is how solo freelancers produce enough content for multi-platform clients without burning out.

**Scheduling Tools:**

- **Buffer** — clean, simple, supports most platforms, free tier available. Best for small accounts.
- **Hootsuite** — more powerful, built-in analytics, better for agency managing 5+ accounts. Paid.
- **Later** — best for Instagram-focused clients, visual calendar view.
- **LinkedIn native scheduler** — always use LinkedIn's own scheduler for LinkedIn posts (better organic reach than third-party tools).

## Lesson 7.3 — Meta Ads (Facebook + Instagram) for International Campaigns

**Duration:** 75 min

**Lesson Content:**

Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram ads via the same platform) is the most widely-used paid social tool for international clients. Almost every digital marketer working with US, UK, or Australian clients will be expected to understand and manage Meta campaigns.

**Meta Business Suite Setup:**

1. Go to [business.facebook.com](http://business.facebook.com) → Create a Business Account
2. Add the client's Facebook Page and Instagram Account
3. Create an Ad Account (Settings → Ad Accounts → Add)
4. Install the Meta Pixel on the client's website (Events Manager → Pixels → Set Up Pixel → use a partner integration like Shopify/WordPress or manual code install)
5. Verify the Pixel is firing: use the "Test Events" tool in Events Manager

**Campaign Objectives — Choose the Right One:**

- **Awareness** — maximises reach and impressions. Use for new brand launches, no direct response goal.
- **Traffic** — drives clicks to a website or landing page. Use for blog traffic, portfolio visits.
- **Engagement** — optimises for likes, comments, shares. Use for building social proof.
- **Leads** — uses a built-in Meta Lead Form (user fills in without leaving the app). Best for B2B and service businesses.
- **Conversions** — optimises for a specific action on your website (purchase, sign-up). Requires Pixel and conversion event set up. Best for e-commerce and landing pages.

**Audience Targeting — The Three Layers:**

1. **Custom Audiences** — people who already know you: website visitors (via Pixel), email list uploads, video viewers, Instagram engagers. These are your warmest audiences and convert at the highest rate.
2. **Lookalike Audiences** — Meta finds people who are similar to your Custom Audience. A 1% Lookalike is the most similar (narrowest but highest quality). Use when you have at least 1,000 people in the source Custom Audience.
3. **Interest/Behaviour Targeting (Cold)** — for brands with no existing audience. Target by interests, demographics, job titles, life events. This is the starting point for new accounts.

**Ad Creative Best Practices:**

- Video ads outperform image ads in most markets — even a simple talking-head video or screen recording
- Hook must appear in the first 3 seconds — most users scroll past by second 4
- Text overlay: keep it under 20% of the image (Meta's old rule — still applies to clarity)
- Always test multiple creatives — run 3–5 ad variations per ad set and let the algorithm find the winner
- Ad copy formula: Problem → Agitate → Solution → CTA

**Budget Management for USD Campaigns:**

- Start with a daily budget of $10–$20 per ad set to gather data
- Do not touch campaigns in the first 7 days — Meta's algorithm is in a "learning phase" and needs 50 optimization events before it stabilises
- Once a winner is identified, scale by increasing budget 20–30% every 3 days (not all at once — resets the learning phase)

LinkedIn Ads are the single most precise B2B targeting tool in digital marketing. You can target by job title, seniority, company name, company size, industry, and skills — simultaneously. No other platform gets this specific about professional attributes.

# Week 8 — Content Marketing & Email Marketing

## Lesson 8.1 — Content Strategy for International Audiences

**Duration:** 60 min

**Lesson Content:**

Content marketing is the practice of creating and distributing valuable, relevant content to attract and retain a clearly defined audience — with the goal of driving profitable action. It is the highest-ROI long-term digital marketing strategy because compound content (a blog post, YouTube video, or podcast episode) keeps generating leads for years after it's created, unlike paid ads that stop the moment you stop spending.

**Why International Clients Invest Heavily in Content:**

US and UK companies allocate 25–40% of their marketing budget to content marketing. The reason: in competitive markets, organic search traffic and thought leadership are the primary drivers of inbound leads at scale. If your client is not producing content, their competitors are taking that traffic.

| Funnel Stage | Goal | Content Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Top of Funnel (TOFU) — Awareness** | Attract new audiences | Blog posts, YouTube videos, social media, podcasts, infographics |
| **Middle of Funnel (MOFU) — Consideration** | Build trust and educate | Case studies, white papers, webinars, email sequences, comparison guides |
| **Bottom of Funnel (BOFU) — Decision** | Convert to leads or customers | Demo videos, testimonials, free trials, ROI calculators, proposal templates |

## Lesson 8.2 — Blog Writing That Ranks and Converts

**Duration:** 60 min

**Lesson Content:**

A blog post that ranks on Google and converts readers into leads must do two things simultaneously: satisfy Google's algorithm and satisfy a human reader's intent. These goals are more aligned than most people think — Google has gotten very good at detecting whether real humans find content useful.

**The Anatomy of a High-Ranking Blog Post:**

1. **Title/H1** — Include the target keyword near the start. Under 60 characters if possible. Make it specific ("How to Hire a Virtual Assistant for Your Business in 2026" outperforms "Hiring a VA Guide").
2. **Meta Description** — 150–155 characters. Include keyword naturally. End with a subtle CTA ("Learn the exact process.").
3. **Introduction (150–200 words)** — Hook in sentence 1 (statistic, question, or bold claim). Acknowledge the reader's problem. State what the article will solve. Do NOT start with "In today's fast-paced world..." — international readers will click away immediately.
4. **H2 Sections (main points)** — Each H2 should contain a related keyword or phrase. Under each H2, write 200–400 words that fully addresses that point. Avoid fluff — every sentence must add information.
5. **Internal Links** — Link to 3–5 other relevant pages on the same site within the body copy.
6. **External Links** — Link out to 1–2 credible sources (not competitors). This signals to Google that you're referencing quality information.
7. **CTA at the end** — What should the reader do next? Book a call? Download a resource? Read another article? Always include one clear next step.

**Writing for International (Non-Indian) Readers:**

- Short paragraphs: 2–4 sentences maximum. Long blocks of text cause drop-off.
- Simple vocabulary: write at a Grade 8 reading level (Hemingway App can check this)
- Active voice throughout: "We increased conversions by 40%" not "Conversions were increased by 40% by our team"
- Avoid Indian English patterns: don't say "kindly", "do the needful", "as per", "revert back"
- Contractions are fine: "you'll", "it's", "we're" — international business writing is conversational

**Using AI to Accelerate Without Losing Quality:**

- Use AI to generate a first-draft outline, then rewrite every section in your own words
- AI drafts are a starting point — never submit unedited AI output to a client
- Use Grammarly to catch grammar issues; use Hemingway App to simplify complex sentences
- Add real examples, specific data, and first-person observations — AI cannot do this

**Word Count Benchmarks:**

- Informational blog posts ("How to X"): 1,200–2,000 words
- Comparison posts ("X vs Y"): 1,500–2,500 words
- Pillar/guide pages: 3,000–5,000 words
- Service pages: 600–1,000 words

## Lesson 8.3 — Email Marketing: List Building to Automation

**Duration:** 75 min

**Lesson Content:**

Email marketing consistently produces the highest ROI of any digital channel — an average of $36 for every $1 spent, according to Litmus research. For international clients, email is not optional — it is a core retention and monetisation channel.

**Platform Overview:**

- **Mailchimp** — most widely used, easy to set up, good free tier (up to 500 contacts), limited automation on free plan. Best for beginners and small lists.
- **Klaviyo** — industry standard for e-commerce (Shopify integration is excellent), powerful segmentation and flow automation, paid from day one. Best for DTC/e-commerce clients.
- **ConvertKit (now Kit)** — preferred by creators, coaches, and course sellers. Clean tagging and automation system. Good free tier.
- **ActiveCampaign** — powerful CRM + email combined, best for B2B and complex automation. Steeper learning curve.

**List Building — The Only Sustainable Method:**

You cannot buy email lists. You build them through:

- **Lead magnets** — a free resource (PDF checklist, template, mini-course, calculator) that the visitor gets in exchange for their email address
- **Pop-up or inline opt-in forms** — on the website, triggered by exit intent or time on page
- **Content upgrades** — a bonus piece of content offered within a blog post (e.g., "Download the full checklist mentioned in this article")
- **Webinars and events** — attendees register with their email

**Welcome Sequence — The Most Important Emails You'll Write:**

The welcome sequence is the automated series of emails triggered the moment someone joins your list. Open rates on welcome emails average 50–80% — far higher than any broadcast email. These are the emails that set the relationship.

A strong 5-email welcome sequence structure:

1. **Email 1 (sent immediately):** Deliver the lead magnet + warm personal welcome. Introduce who you/the brand are in 3 sentences.
2. **Email 2 (Day 2):** Share the biggest mistake your audience makes. Position yourself as someone who understands their problem.
3. **Email 3 (Day 4):** Social proof — a client story, testimonial, or result. Specific numbers make this real.
4. **Email 4 (Day 6):** Teach something valuable for free. A quick win. This builds trust before any offer.
5. **Email 5 (Day 8):** Soft introduction to your offer/service. Not a hard sell — frame it as the logical next step for someone who wants more.

- Open rate: 20–35% is healthy (varies by industry)
- Click-through rate: 2–5%
- Unsubscribe rate: under 0.5% per send (higher means list is being sent irrelevant content)

**GDPR Compliance (for UK/EU clients — mandatory):**

- You must have explicit consent to email someone (pre-ticked boxes are not allowed)
- Every email must have an unsubscribe link
- You must have a clear Privacy Policy stating how data is used
- Data must be stored in GDPR-compliant platforms (all major email tools are compliant)
- Failure to comply: fines up to €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover

# Week 9 — Analytics, Reporting & Optimisation

## Lesson 9.1 — Google Analytics 4 (GA4) Fundamentals

**Duration:** 75 min

**Lesson Content:**

GA4 is Google's current analytics platform (Universal Analytics was shut down in 2023). Every international client's website should have GA4 installed. Your ability to read GA4 and explain what the data means is a core competency that directly determines how much clients trust you.

**GA4 vs Universal Analytics — The Key Difference:**

Universal Analytics tracked "sessions" and "page views" as its core unit. GA4 tracks "events" — every interaction (page view, scroll, click, form submission, video play) is an event. This gives far more data about how users actually behave, not just which pages they visited.

**The 5 GA4 Reports You Must Know:**

1. **Acquisition Report** (Reports → Acquisition → Traffic Acquisition)

Shows where your visitors are coming from: Organic Search, Direct, Paid Search, Social, Email, Referral. This is the first report to check when a client asks "where are my visitors coming from?" or "is our SEO working?"

1. **Engagement Report** (Reports → Engagement → Pages and Screens)

Shows which pages are getting the most views and how long people spend on them. "Engagement Rate" in GA4 replaces "Bounce Rate" — an engaged session is one that lasts 10+ seconds, has a conversion event, or has 2+ page views. An engagement rate above 50% is healthy.

1. **Conversions Report** (Reports → Engagement → Conversions)

Shows how many times a conversion event fired. You must set up conversion events (e.g., form_submit, purchase, sign_up) for this report to have meaningful data. Without conversion tracking, you cannot prove the value of your work.

1. **User Demographics** (Reports → User → User Attributes)

Shows country, city, age range, gender, and device type. Useful for confirming your target market is actually visiting, and for identifying unexpected audiences.

1. **Real-Time Report** (Reports → Real-Time)

Shows who is on the site right now. Useful for verifying that tracking is working correctly after a change, or for monitoring live campaign launches.

**Setting Up Conversion Events in GA4:**

1. Go to Admin → Events
2. Find the event you want to mark as a conversion (e.g., "form_submit" or "purchase")
3. Toggle "Mark as conversion" — it will now appear in Conversions reports

If your key event doesn't exist yet, you need to create a custom event via GTM (covered in Lesson 9.2).

**What to Look For When a Client Says "Traffic Is Down":**

- Check the Acquisition report: which channel dropped? (Only SEO down = Google algorithm update; only Paid down = budget issue or ad rejected; all channels down = website technical problem or seasonality)
- Check the date comparison (use the comparison toggle to compare to previous period or previous year)
- Check the Engagement rate: did traffic drop but engagement rate go up? (Better quality visitors, fewer but more relevant)
- Check if any pages lost significant views (could be an indexing issue)

## Lesson 9.2 — Google Tag Manager (GTM) Basics

**Duration:** 60 min

**Lesson Content:**

Google Tag Manager is a tool that lets you add and manage tracking code on a website without editing the website's code directly. This matters because: (a) it's faster — you can deploy tracking in minutes without a developer, (b) it's safer — mistakes don't touch the live website code, and (c) it's what agencies use — if you don't know GTM, you will be blocked from doing proper analytics work.

**Core Concepts:**

- **Container** — the GTM code snippet installed once on every page of the site. Everything else lives inside the container.
- **Tags** — the code you want to fire (e.g., a GA4 tracking code, a Meta Pixel, a Google Ads conversion tag)
- **Triggers** — the rules that determine WHEN a tag fires (e.g., on every page view, when a button is clicked, when a form is submitted)
- **Variables** — stored values that tags and triggers can reference (e.g., the URL of the current page, the value of a click)

**Step-by-Step: Setting Up GA4 via GTM**

1. Go to [tagmanager.google.com](http://tagmanager.google.com) → Create Account → Create Container (select Web)
2. Copy the GTM container snippet → install in the `<head>` and `<body>` of your website (or use a CMS plugin like GTM4WP for WordPress)
3. In GTM: New Tag → Tag Type: Google Analytics: GA4 Configuration → paste your GA4 Measurement ID (format: G-XXXXXXXX)
4. Trigger: All Pages (Page View)
5. Save → Submit → Publish
6. Verify: go to your website, open GTM Preview mode (click Preview in GTM) → confirm GA4 Configuration tag fires on every page

**Creating a Custom Event Tag (Form Submission Tracking):**

1. New Tag → Tag Type: Google Analytics: GA4 Event → Event Name: `form_submit`
2. Trigger: New Trigger → Trigger Type: Form Submission → enable "Check validation" (only fires if the form was actually submitted successfully)
3. Save → Preview → test by submitting a form on the site → confirm the tag fires
4. In GA4: Admin → Events → you should see `form_submit` appearing within 24 hours. Mark it as a conversion.

**Common GTM Mistakes:**

- Forgetting to **Publish** after making changes (changes only go live when published)
- Creating duplicate tags that fire GA4 pageview events twice (inflates traffic data)
- Not using Preview mode before publishing (always test before going live)
- Installing GTM AND pasting GA4 code directly on the site (results in double tracking)

## Lesson 9.4 — Optimisation: Making Data-Driven Decisions

**Duration:** 60 min

**Lesson Content:**

Optimisation is the process of using data to systematically improve performance. The trap most junior marketers fall into is making changes based on instinct or client opinions rather than data. This lesson gives you the framework for doing it properly.

**A/B Testing — The Only Correct Way to Know What Works:**

An A/B test compares two versions of something (an ad, a landing page, an email subject line) to determine which performs better. You show version A to 50% of your audience and version B to the other 50%, then measure which produced the better result.

Rules for valid A/B tests:

- **Change only ONE variable at a time** — if you change the headline AND the image, you don't know which caused the difference
- **Run tests long enough** — minimum 7 days, ideally 14 days, until you have statistical significance
- **Statistical significance** — you need enough data before declaring a winner. Use a free calculator like [abtestguide.com/calc](http://abtestguide.com/calc) — aim for 95% confidence
- **Document everything** — keep a testing log: what was tested, hypothesis, result, action taken

**What to Test First — Highest-Impact Areas:**

1. **Email subject lines** — even a 5% improvement in open rate has a compound effect over time. Test: question vs statement, short vs long, personalised vs generic.
2. **Landing page headline** — the single biggest lever on conversion rate. Test: problem-focused vs outcome-focused.
3. **Ad creative** — test video vs image, or different hook lines in the first 3 seconds.
4. **CTA copy** — "Book a Free Call" vs "Get My Free Strategy Session" can produce wildly different click rates.
5. **Send time for emails** — Tuesday/Wednesday 9–11am tend to perform well in US/UK markets, but test against your specific list.

**CRO (Conversion Rate Optimisation) — The 5-Minute Audit:**

When a landing page is underperforming (opt-in rate below 20%), check these in order:

1. **Headline** — does it clearly state what the visitor gets and why they should care?
2. **Above the fold** — can the visitor see the CTA without scrolling?
3. **Page speed** — run through PageSpeed Insights. Every 1-second delay costs ~7% in conversions.
4. **Form length** — are you asking for too much information?
5. **Social proof** — is there a testimonial or trust signal near the CTA?

**Hotjar for Heatmaps:**

Hotjar shows you where users click, scroll, and drop off on your pages. Install via a GTM tag (same process as GA4). Check the scroll depth — if 60% of users never scroll past the hero section, your most important content needs to move up.

# Week 10 — Putting It All Together: Full Campaign Simulation

## Lesson 10.1 — Integrated Campaign Planning

**Duration:** 75 min

**Lesson Content:**

A multi-channel campaign is not the sum of individual channel activities — it is a coordinated system where every channel plays a specific role in moving a prospect from first awareness to purchase. This is the strategic thinking that clients pay premium rates for.

**How the Channels Work Together:**

- **SEO** sets the long-term organic foundation — attracts traffic searching for solutions
- **Paid Ads (Google/Meta)** captures immediate demand and fills the funnel with new leads quickly
- **Social Media (Organic)** builds brand trust, stays top-of-mind, and warms cold audiences
- **Content Marketing** creates the assets that SEO, social, and email all depend on
- **Email Marketing** converts leads into customers and turns customers into repeat buyers

Think of it as a system: Paid Ads → Landing Page → Email List → Email Sequence → Sale. SEO and Organic Social feed the top of that system over time.

**90-Day Integrated Campaign Structure:**

- **Month 1 — Foundation:** Audit existing assets, set up tracking (GA4, GTM, Pixel), produce foundational content (key blog posts, lead magnet), warm audiences via organic social
