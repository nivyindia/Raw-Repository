# P1.2.1 — Content Pillars & Calendar System: Building a 90-Day Content Engine

**Learning Objectives:** Students can design a 5-pillar content framework tailored to a brand, translate it into a 90-day content calendar, and set up a reusable Notion content-calendar template.

**Outline:**

1. Hook — "Posting randomly is why most brand accounts plateau at the same follower count for years. A pillar system turns content from guesswork into a repeatable engine."
2. What a content pillar actually is — a recurring theme tied to an audience need and a business goal, not just a "post type"
3. The 5-Pillar Framework — Educate, Entertain, Inspire, Connect/Community, Promote/Convert — with worked examples for a D2C brand, a B2B SaaS, and a local service business
4. Mapping pillars to platforms and formats — what suits a Reel vs a carousel vs a static post vs a Story
5. Building the 90-day calendar — posting cadence per pillar, seasonal/trend slots, and a healthy evergreen-vs-timely content ratio
6. **Live demo:** Build a Notion content-calendar template live — properties for Pillar, Platform, Format, Status, Caption Draft, Asset Link
7. Batching workflow — content sprints and repurposing one shoot/recording session into multiple weeks of content
8. Checkpoint quiz (Sub-Topic Checkpoint template, QW-05)

**Recording notes:** Use one consistent example brand throughout the module (e.g. a fictional D2C skincare brand) so students see the pillar → calendar → caption pipeline end to end. Ship the Notion calendar as a duplicate-able template — this is the module's downloadable resource.

**Practical Exam tie-in:** Task 1 of the P1.2 Module Practical Exam — students build a 5-pillar framework plus a 30-day content calendar (a working subset of the 90-day system) for a brand of their choice, using the provided template.

# P1.2.2 — Instagram Deep Dive: Reels, Stories & Shopping in 2026

**Learning Objectives:** Students can build an Instagram growth strategy that combines Reels algorithm mechanics, carousel design for saves/shares, Story-based funnels, and native Shopping features.

**Outline:**

1. Hook — "Instagram in 2026 rewards watch time and shares more than likes — if your strategy is still 'post and pray for likes,' you're optimising for the wrong metric."
2. How the Reels algorithm evaluates content — average watch time, replays, and shares weighted above likes; why the first 1–3 seconds (the hook) decide everything
3. Carousel strategy — designing for the "save this for later" behaviour, swipe-through hooks, and using carousels to drive saves and shares
4. Story funnels — sequencing Stories (polls, quizzes, countdowns, link stickers) to move a viewer from awareness to a click
5. Close Friends list strategy — using it for VIP offers, behind-the-scenes content, and community-building as a soft "email list" alternative
6. Instagram Shopping integration — product tags, Shop tab setup, and checkout basics for D2C brands
7. **Live demo:** Review 3 real (anonymised) Reels — break down hooks, pacing, and captions, then redesign one as a carousel
8. Analytics check — reading watch time, shares, and profile visits instead of fixating on likes
9. Checkpoint quiz

**Recording notes:** Source 2–3 real Reels (screen-recorded, brand names anonymised if needed) for the live breakdown — this is the most engaging segment. Keep the Shopping section practical: a high-level walkthrough of Meta Commerce Manager setup, since most students will manage an existing setup rather than build one from scratch.

**Practical Exam tie-in:** Task 2 — students storyboard one Reel (hook + 3 key beats) and one carousel (5–7 slides) for their chosen brand, applying the hook-first and save-worthy principles taught in this module.

# P1.2.3 — LinkedIn B2B Strategy: Personal Branding to Pipeline

**Learning Objectives:** Students can build a LinkedIn content strategy spanning both personal branding and company-page growth, and explain how LinkedIn content fits into a B2B lead-generation funnel.

**Outline:**

1. Hook — "A founder's personal LinkedIn post regularly outperforms their company page by 10–20x reach. Here's why — and how to use that."
2. Personal branding on LinkedIn — profile optimisation (headline, banner, About section, Featured section) and positioning for a specific audience
3. Content formats that perform — text posts with strong hooks, document/carousel "PDF slide" posts, native video, and polls
4. Company page growth — posting cadence, employee advocacy (getting team members to engage and share), and Showcase Pages
5. LinkedIn newsletters — setup, cadence, and how they create a recurring "appointment" with an audience
6. Thought leadership frameworks — turning expertise into posts using the "lesson from a project" format, contrarian takes, and data-backed posts
7. Sales Navigator basics — what it is, who needs it, and a conceptual walkthrough of lead lists and saved searches
8. **Live demo:** Rewrite a generic company announcement as a personal thought-leadership post
9. Checkpoint quiz

**Recording notes:** Keep this aspirational but practical — many students are job-seeking or freelancing, so frame personal branding as directly useful to their own careers, not just "the brand." Treat Sales Navigator conceptually since it's a paid tool: focus on what it enables rather than a full screen walkthrough.

**Practical Exam tie-in:** Task 3 — students write 3 LinkedIn posts (one personal thought-leadership post, one document/carousel outline, and one company-page post) for their chosen brand or their own profile.

# P1.2.4 — YouTube Channel Growth System: Beyond Just Uploading Videos

**Learning Objectives:** Students can configure a YouTube channel for discovery and retention using end screens, cards, chapter markers, the Community tab, and a Shorts-to-long-form funnel strategy.

**Outline:**

1. Hook — "Two channels can post the exact same video — one gets 500 views, the other gets 50,000. The difference is almost never the video itself. It's the system around it."
2. Channel setup essentials — channel art, About-section SEO keywords, channel trailer, and playlists as a navigation tool
3. End screens and cards — driving session watch time by linking to the next best video, not just "subscribe"
4. Chapter markers — improving viewer navigation while giving Google more context to rank specific timestamps
5. Community tab — using polls, behind-the-scenes posts, and teasers to stay visible between uploads
6. Shorts strategy — how Shorts feed discovery into the channel, and a "Shorts → long-form" funnel of teaser Shorts pointing to full videos
7. **Live demo:** Set up end screens and a chapter list on a sample video, then draft 3 Community tab post ideas
8. Upload workflow checklist — the right sequencing of title, thumbnail, description, and tags for a new upload
9. Checkpoint quiz

**Recording notes:** Use a screen-recorded walkthrough of YouTube Studio for the live demo — this is one of the most-requested "show me the actual screen" topics. Emphasise that growth is a *system* (upload workflow + end screens + Shorts + Community tab working together), not any single tactic.

**Practical Exam tie-in:** Task 4 — students complete an "upload readiness checklist" for a sample video: a title/thumbnail/description draft, 3 chapter markers, an end-screen plan, and 2 Community tab post ideas.

# P1.2.5 — Twitter/X Marketing: Threads, Engagement Loops & Community Building

**Learning Objectives:** Students can write a high-performing thread, design a basic engagement-loop strategy, and evaluate whether X Premium features are worth adopting for a given brand or creator.

**Outline:**

1. Hook — "On X, one good thread can do more for a brand in a week than a month of regular posts. But most threads fail in the first line."
2. Anatomy of a thread that gets read — hook-tweet structures (curiosity gap, bold claim, numbered promise), pacing, and the "payoff" tweet
3. Engagement loops — replying to your own thread, quote-tweeting past content, and the "warm-up" habit of engaging in relevant conversations before posting
4. X Premium features overview — what's genuinely useful for marketers (longer posts, edit, analytics) vs what's not essential
5. Community building on X — the Communities feature and building a niche following through consistent participation, not just broadcasting
6. **Live demo:** Take a single idea and turn it into both a standalone post and a 6-tweet thread — compare the two structures side by side
7. What to avoid — engagement-bait and controversy-for-reach tactics that damage brand trust (ties back to ethics)
8. Checkpoint quiz

**Recording notes:** Stay realistic about X's volatile algorithm changes — frame the principles (hooks, engagement loops, community participation) as durable even as specific features shift. Avoid over-promising "going viral."

**Practical Exam tie-in:** Task 5 (final task) of the P1.2 Module Practical Exam — students write one 5–7 tweet thread for their chosen brand, applying the hook structure taught in this module.
