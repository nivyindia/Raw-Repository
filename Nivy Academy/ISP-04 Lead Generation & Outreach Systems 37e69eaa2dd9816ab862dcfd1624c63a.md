# Week 15 — Understanding the International Client & Market

## Lesson 15.1 — Ideal Client Profile (ICP) for International Work

**Content:** What an ICP is and why it determines everything else, the 5 ICP dimensions (industry, company size, geography, role, pain point), building your ICP document, difference between a lead, prospect, MQL, and SQL, most profitable international client profiles for Indian freelancers (US SaaS $1M–10M ARR, UK e-commerce, AU professional services, CA coaches)

**Daily Task:** Write your ICP document. Include: industry, company size range, geography, decision-maker title, top 3 pain points, budget signals, and where they hang out online. Submit.

## Lesson 15.2 — Where to Find International Clients

**Content:** Platform breakdown: Upwork (best for beginners), LinkedIn (best for B2B outreach), [Apollo.io](http://Apollo.io) (best for cold email), Twitter/X (best for thought leadership inbound), [Clutch.co](http://Clutch.co) (agency directory), AngelList/Wellfound (startups), G2/Capterra (SaaS companies), ProductHunt (new startups), Reddit (community-based), Fiverr Pro (productised services)

**Daily Task:** For your ICP, identify the top 3 platforms where your ideal clients are most active. Write a 1-page strategy for how you will use each platform to reach them.

## Lesson 15.3 — [Apollo.io](http://Apollo.io) Masterclass: Building Targeted Lists

**Content:** [Apollo.io](http://Apollo.io) setup and navigation, building search filters (job title, company size, location, industry, revenue, technology used), exporting lead lists, email verification, enriching contact data, Apollo sequences overview

**SOP — Apollo Lead List Building:**

1. Log in to [Apollo.io](http://Apollo.io)
2. Go to People Search
3. Apply filters: Job Title = [target role], Location = [target country], Company Size = [range], Industry = [your niche]
4. Review results — remove irrelevant entries manually
5. Save to a list (name it: [Niche] – [Date] – [Country])
6. Export to CSV with: Name, Title, Company, Email, LinkedIn URL, Phone (if available)
7. Run emails through Apollo verifier or NeverBounce
8. Upload to your outreach CRM or sequence tool

**Daily Task:** Build a list of 50 qualified leads for your ICP using Apollo. Export to a spreadsheet. Verify emails. Submit the list.

## Lesson 15.4 — LinkedIn Lead Generation System

**Content:** LinkedIn Sales Navigator overview (trial available), Boolean search for finding decision-makers, connection request strategy, what to say in a connection note (character limit: 300), how to find emails of LinkedIn connections, LinkedIn outreach sequencing (connect → follow-up message → value post → soft pitch)

**Daily Task:** Send 20 connection requests to ICP-matched LinkedIn profiles with personalised notes. Screenshot all 20 sent requests with notes visible. Submit.

**Weekly Quiz 15 + Weekly Target:** 50-lead Apollo list + 20 LinkedIn connection requests sent.

# Week 16 — Cold Email Outreach System

## Lesson 16.1 — Cold Email Psychology & Deliverability

**Content:** Why most cold emails fail (wrong subject line, too long, no personalisation, wrong CTA), the psychology of a cold email that gets replies, sender reputation and deliverability (SPF, DKIM, DMARC setup), warm-up tools (Mailreach, Lemwarm), sending limits (start at 20/day, scale to 100/day over 4 weeks)

**Daily Task:** Set up email DNS records (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) for a domain (or document the steps if you don’t own a domain yet). Submit screenshot + verification.

## Lesson 16.2 — Writing Cold Emails That Get Replies

**Content:** The AIDA framework for cold email (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action), subject line formulas that work, the 5-sentence cold email rule, personalisation at scale ({{first_name}}, {{company}}, {{specific observation}}), the soft CTA (not "hop on a call" — ask a yes/no question instead), social proof insertion, follow-up sequence structure (7-touch over 14 days)

**Email Templates Provided:**

- Template A: Problem-Aware Lead
- Template B: Referral to Resource
- Template C: Compliment + Question
- Template D: Case Study Teaser
- Template E: Direct Pitch (only for warm leads)

**Daily Task:** Write 3 personalised cold emails to 3 real leads from your Apollo list. Not templates — personalised with observations about their business. Submit all 3 with personalisation notes.

## Lesson 16.3 — Cold Email Tools & Automation

**Content:** [Instantly.ai](http://Instantly.ai) (best for beginners), Lemlist (best for personalisation with images/videos), Woodpecker (enterprise), Smartlead, how to set up a sequence, A/B testing subject lines, tracking opens and replies, when to stop following up

**SOP — Cold Email Campaign Launch ([Instantly.ai](http://Instantly.ai)):**

1. Create account → Connect sending email domain
2. Upload lead list (CSV)
3. Create campaign → Name: [Niche] – [Month] – [Country]
4. Write Sequence: Email 1 (Day 0) → Follow-up 1 (Day 3) → Follow-up 2 (Day 7) → Break-up email (Day 14)
5. Set daily sending limit: 20/day for weeks 1–2, increase to 50/day weeks 3–4
6. Enable tracking: opens, clicks, replies
7. Monitor daily: reply → pause lead → move to CRM → book call

**Daily Task:** Set up one cold email campaign in [Instantly.ai](http://Instantly.ai) or Lemlist (or document setup as a step-by-step walkthrough if no live account). Submit Loom walkthrough.

## Lesson 16.4 — Handling Replies: From Interest to Booked Call

**Content:** Reply management: positive, neutral, objection, unsubscribe. How to respond to each in under 10 minutes. The goal of every reply is ONE thing: book the discovery call. How to move a curious reply into a confirmed calendar booking.

**Daily Task:** Write response templates for 5 reply types: (1) Interested but busy, (2) Tell me more, (3) We already have someone, (4) What are your rates?, (5) Not interested. Submit all 5.

**Weekly Quiz 16 + Weekly Target:** Cold email campaign set up + reply templates written.

# Week 17 — LinkedIn DM & Social Outreach

## Lesson 17.1 — LinkedIn DM Outreach System

**Content:** The LinkedIn DM sequence that doesn’t feel spammy (connect → warm message → value drop → soft pitch), the difference between message 1 and message 3, using Loom videos in DMs for a 3x reply rate lift, when to pitch vs when to build relationship, LinkedIn voice notes as a differentiator

**Daily Task:** Write a 5-message LinkedIn DM sequence for your ICP. Messages must feel natural and progressive — not salesy until message 4 or 5. Submit.

## Lesson 17.2 — Social Selling: Instagram, Twitter & Facebook Groups

**Content:** Finding and engaging in relevant online communities (Facebook Groups for business owners, Twitter spaces, Reddit communities), commenting strategy (add value before pitching), DM from engagement (not cold), how to move a social relationship to a business conversation

**Daily Task:** Join 3 relevant Facebook Groups or Reddit communities for your target ICP. Make 5 genuine, value-adding comments in each. Screenshot submissions.

## Lesson 17.3 — Platform-Specific Outreach: Upwork & Fiverr

**Content:** Winning proposals on Upwork (structure, personalisation, portfolio attachments, pricing), Upwork profile optimisation for international clients, Fiverr Pro qualification, writing Fiverr gig descriptions that rank and convert, responding to buyer requests

**SOP — Writing a Winning Upwork Proposal:**

1. Read the job post 3 times
2. Identify the real problem (not just the task listed)
3. Open with: address the problem directly in 1 sentence
4. Line 2–3: Explain your relevant experience or approach
5. Line 4: Specific question about their project (shows you read it)
6. Line 5: Your process in 3 steps
7. Attach 1–2 relevant portfolio samples
8. CTA: "Happy to discuss further in a quick call"
9. Do not include rates in the proposal body — address in follow-up

**Daily Task:** Apply to 5 real Upwork jobs (or write 5 proposals for fictional job postings provided). Submit all 5 proposals with a self-critique for each.

## Lesson 17.4 — Referral & Warm Introduction Strategy

**Content:** How 80% of high-value contracts come from referrals, building a referral network even before your first client, how to ask for referrals without being awkward, LinkedIn introduction requests, strategic partnerships with complementary freelancers

**Daily Task:** Write a referral request message for 3 people in your network who know potential clients. Personalise each one. Submit.

**Weekly Quiz 17 + Weekly Target:** 5 Upwork proposals submitted + LinkedIn DM sequence written.

## Lesson 18.1 — Managing Your Lead Pipeline

## Lessons 18.2–18.4 — Live Outreach Sprint (3 days) *(enriched from NA-AC-03 Outreach Sprint SOP)*

**The Sprint — Day by Day:**

- **Day 1:** Send 20 cold emails via your Apollo sequence (Day 0 step) + 10 LinkedIn connection requests with personalised notes + 3 value-adding comments on prospects' posts
- **Day 2:** Reply to any responses within 4 hours + send 10 more LinkedIn DMs/follow-ups + log every action in your CRM the same day
- **Day 3:** Review pipeline, update CRM, complete your Sprint Performance Report (below)

**SOP — How to Count "Contacts Reached":**

- **Email:** 1 contact reached per person per sequence (not per individual email step within the sequence)
- **LinkedIn:** A connection request sent = 1 contact. A follow-up message to an accepted connection = additional touchpoint, not a new contact
- **WhatsApp** (use only for India/UAE/SE Asia prospects — avoid for US/UK/AU markets, where it reads as intrusive): 1 message = 1 contact
- **Sprint target:** 100 unique people contacted across all channels combined

**Reply-Handling Scripts — use immediately when someone replies:**

- **Positive reply** ("Tell me more" / "Let's chat"): Reply within 4 hours → thank them briefly → offer 3 specific call-time slots in their timezone → send a Calendly invite once agreed → log as "Meeting Booked"
- **Neutral reply** ("Not right now" / "Maybe later"): Respond warmly, offer to follow up in 30/60 days → log as "Nurture" with a reminder date
- **Negative reply** ("Not interested" / "Remove me"): Thank them politely, wish them well → log as "Not Interested — Closed" → do not follow up again

**Benchmarks:** Open rate 20–30% (below 15% = subject line problem) · Reply rate 2–5% (above 8% = excellent) · Positive reply rate 30–50% of replies · LinkedIn acceptance 20–35% (below 15% = profile or note problem).

**A/B Testing rule:** Change only ONE variable at a time (subject line, opening line, or CTA). Split your list, run for 7 days, keep the winner.
