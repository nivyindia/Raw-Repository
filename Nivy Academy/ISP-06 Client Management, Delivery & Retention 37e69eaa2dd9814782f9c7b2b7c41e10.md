# Week 23 — Client Onboarding & Professional Delivery

## Lesson 23.1 — The Client Onboarding System

**Content:** Why how you onboard a client determines whether you retain them, the first 72-hour experience (this is when clients decide if they made the right choice), onboarding checklist, welcome email structure, client kickoff call agenda, setting expectations in writing, collecting assets (logins, brand guidelines, previous reports, audience data)

**Client Onboarding SOP:**

1. Contract signed → Invoice sent → Payment confirmed
2. Send Welcome Email within 24 hours: warmth, what happens next, your direct contact
3. Share Client Onboarding Form (Google Form): collect logins, brand voice, goals, reporting preferences, communication preferences
4. Set up Client Folder in Google Drive: share with client
5. Create Client Project Tracker in Notion/Trello: share link
6. Schedule Kickoff Call within 3–7 days of signing
7. Kickoff Call: review goals, confirm deliverables, agree communication cadence, agree reporting format
8. Send kickoff summary email within 2 hours of call
9. Begin work on Day 1 of contract start date

**Daily Task:** Build a complete client onboarding kit: welcome email template, onboarding Google Form with 10 questions, client Google Drive folder structure, Notion project tracker template. Submit all.

## Lesson 23.2 — Communication Standards During a Project

**Content:** The 3 rules of international client communication (proactive, brief, solution-focused), weekly status update email structure, when to send a Loom update vs a written email, how to communicate when things go wrong (own it, give a plan, resolve it fast), the 24-hour response rule in practice

**Content:** File naming conventions, delivery formats expected by international clients, how to present work (never just send a file — always explain what it is and what to do with it), getting feedback without going back and forth 10 times (use a structured feedback form), revision policy enforcement

# Week 24 — Retention, Upselling, Referrals & Graduation

## Lesson 24.1 — Client Retention: Keeping Clients for 12+ Months

**Content:** Why retaining clients is 5x cheaper than finding new ones, the retention mindset (always be adding value, always be improving), monthly check-in culture, surprising clients with small extras (a quick audit, an industry article, a new idea they didn’t ask for), warning signs a client is about to leave and how to address it before they do

**Daily Task:** For a fictional 3-month client, design a retention plan for months 4–12. What extra value will you deliver each month? How will you stay top of mind? Submit as a retention roadmap.

## Lesson 24.2 — Upselling & Expanding Contracts

**Content:** The 3 upsell moments (end of month review, when they share a win, when you identify a new problem), how to propose an expansion without feeling pushy, the "natural next step" framing, transitioning a project-based client to a retainer, service expansion examples (add email marketing to an SEO client, add LinkedIn ads to a content client)

**Daily Task:** Write 3 upsell proposals for 3 different fictional client scenarios. Each must feel like a natural recommendation, not a sales pitch. Submit.

## Lesson 24.3 — Generating Referrals from Happy Clients

**Content:** When to ask for referrals (after a win, not at the start), how to ask (the exact script), what to offer as a referral incentive (credit, gift, discount on next month), building a referral system into your business, how to make it easy for clients to refer you (pre-written introduction email they can forward)

**SOP — Referral Request:**

1. Wait for a clear client win (metric improved, project completed, positive feedback received)
2. Respond to their positive message: acknowledge the win, thank them
3. Add: "By the way, if you know anyone else who might benefit from [specific result], I’d love an introduction. I’m only taking on [X] new clients this quarter."
4. If they agree: send them a pre-written introduction email they can forward with one click
5. Follow up if no introduction is made within 2 weeks (once, gently)

**Daily Task:** Write a referral request message for a fictional client who just told you their organic traffic went up 40% this month. Also write the pre-written introduction email they could forward. Submit both.

## Lesson 24.4 — Programme Graduation & Your 90-Day Launch Plan

Setting an income target and reverse-engineering it.

# Week 25 — Delivery Systems & AI-Accelerated Output

*(Merged from NA-AC-04 Week 11 — Execution & Scaling)*

## Lesson 25.1 — Building a Repeatable Delivery System

**Content:** The biggest mistake freelancers make is treating every client as a unique project from scratch. The highest-earning freelancers build systems they run for every client: one onboarding process, one set of service SOPs, one weekly schedule, one monthly reporting structure. This is what makes scaling from 1 to 3–5 clients possible without burning out.

**A delivery system means:**

- Every client goes through the same onboarding process (same form, same checklist, same welcome email structure)
- Every service has a documented SOP so it can be done consistently
- Every week has a fixed structure: Monday plan / Tuesday–Thursday delivery / Friday report and review
- Every month ends with a structured report and review call

**Daily Task:** Map out a "week in the life" of managing one international client. What happens on Monday? Tuesday? Wednesday? Thursday? Friday? Write it as a day-by-day schedule — this becomes the backbone of your Client Delivery SOP. Submit.

## Lesson 25.2 — Using AI to Accelerate Delivery Without Losing Quality

**Content:** AI does not replace your thinking — it accelerates your execution. Used correctly, it can cut content and reporting time by 50–70%.

**High-ROI AI use cases in client delivery:**

- **Content creation:** Brief AI → get 5 post drafts → edit 2 → publish. Time saved: ~2 hours/week
- **Email copywriting:** Feed AI the client's product + target audience → get subject lines + email drafts → edit and personalise → send
- **Research:** "Summarise the top 3 competitors for [client] in [market]" → AI starting point to refine
- **Monthly reports:** Paste raw metrics into Claude → "Write a plain-English summary for a non-technical client" → edit for accuracy and voice
- **Proposal drafts:** Feed AI the client brief + your services → get a first draft → personalise

**What AI cannot do:**

- Build genuine relationships with clients
- Make strategic decisions requiring business context
- Produce verified data — always check any numbers AI gives you
- Replace genuine outreach personalisation

**Daily Task:** Use Claude or ChatGPT to draft a monthly performance report for a fictional client. Use these metrics: 1,200 emails sent, 8.2% reply rate, 12 calls booked, 3 proposals sent, 1 client closed at £600/month. Edit the raw AI output to sound like you. Submit both the unedited AI output and your final version.

## Lesson 25.3 — Managing Multiple Clients Without Burning Out

**Content:** Once you have 2–3 clients, workload management becomes critical. The solution is block scheduling and a clear "no-overcommit" rule.

**The 3-client management system:**

- Each client has a dedicated Notion workspace
- You work on clients in time blocks, not simultaneously: "Monday morning = Client A / Monday afternoon = Client B"
- A brief weekly check-in message is sent to every client on Friday (2–3 sentences — even if there's nothing dramatic to report)
- Never accept a new client unless you have documented how you will deliver for them

**Red flags you are overcommitted:**

- Missing deadlines or sending apology emails
- Daily Log shows fewer than 5 tasks completed most days
- CRM and Notion workspaces are not being updated
- Feeling anxious every Monday about client work

If you hit red flags: notify your mentor immediately. Do not accept new clients. Stabilise existing relationships first.

**Daily Task:** Write a capacity plan: how many clients can you realistically manage right now, based on your available hours per day? What systems would you need to handle one more? Submit as a short paragraph.

## Lesson 25.4 — Monthly Retainers: Building Recurring Income

**Content:** A retainer is a monthly agreement where a client pays a fixed fee for an ongoing set of deliverables. Retainers are the foundation of a sustainable freelance income: predictable revenue, deeper client relationships, less constant selling.

**How to convert a project client to a retainer:**

After the first project or first month of results: *"The results so far are [specific result]. To build on this consistently, I'd recommend moving to a monthly retainer. Here's what that would include: [deliverables]. The investment is $[price]/month."*

**Retainer pricing formula:**

- Estimate total hours per month for that client (e.g., 20 hrs)
- Multiply by your target hourly rate (e.g., 20 × $25 = $500/month minimum)
- Add a 20% management premium for coordination, reporting, and calls = $600/month
- Round to the nearest clean number: $600/month

**Daily Task:** Design a retainer package for one client type in your niche. Name it, list every monthly deliverable, price it using the formula above, and write the conversion pitch you would use to move a one-off client onto it. Submit.

## Lesson 26.4 — Optional Advanced Module: Delegating to a Sub-VA

*(Advanced topic — for students who already have 3+ clients or are Accelerated Entry graduates)*

**Content:** Once you are consistently delivering for 2–3 clients, you have the option to bring in a sub-VA to handle execution tasks while you focus on strategy and client relationships. This is how freelancers scale beyond their own time.

**When you are ready to delegate:**

- You have at least 2 paying retainer clients
- You have documented SOPs for every service you deliver (from Lesson 25.1)
- You are turning down work because of capacity, not because of skills
- You have a quality-checking system (you review before anything goes to the client)

**What to delegate first:** Repetitive, rules-based tasks with clear SOPs — prospect list building, social media scheduling, basic report formatting, inbox management. Never delegate strategy, client communication, or discovery calls.

**Where to find sub-VAs:** Nivy Academy alumni network (first choice), Upwork (junior profiles, filtered by ISP-relevant skills), or Fiverr for one-off tasks.

**Note:** This content is optional and does not form part of the graduation assessment. It is provided here for students who are ready to build beyond the solo freelancer model.
