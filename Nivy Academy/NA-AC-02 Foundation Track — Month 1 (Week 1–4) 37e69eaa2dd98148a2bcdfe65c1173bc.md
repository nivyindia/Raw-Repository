## Module 1 — Global Market Fundamentals (Week 1–2)

### Week 1: The Global Opportunity

**Week 1 Learning Goal:** By Friday, you can explain the difference between Indian and international clients, name 5 global niches, and write a structured opportunity report.

---

**Monday — Lesson 1.1: What Is Digital Marketing in a Global Context?**

Digital marketing means promoting businesses online — through search engines, social media, email, and paid ads. In the global context, you are not competing for local businesses paying ₹5,000/month. You are competing for international clients paying $500–$5,000/month for the same work.

Key concepts:

- The difference between domestic and international digital marketing scope
- What international clients actually buy: results (leads, sales, traffic), not hours
- Why geography no longer matters: a student in Kanpur can manage a client in California
- The 3 ways to work internationally: freelance (direct), agency (as a contractor), or remote employment

**Daily Task (Monday):** Write a 5-sentence answer to: "Why would an international client hire someone from India instead of someone local?" Post in your Notion workspace.

---

**Tuesday — Lesson 1.2: The 5 Global Niches**

The 5 highest-demand niches for digital marketing services internationally:

1. **E-commerce** — Online stores selling physical or digital products. Need: traffic, ads, email sequences, product listings. Platforms: Shopify, WooCommerce, Amazon
2. **SaaS (Software as a Service)** — Tech companies with subscription products. Need: content marketing, SEO, LinkedIn lead gen, demos booked. High budgets, long-term contracts
3. **Coaches & Consultants** — Business, life, health coaches. Need: funnels, webinars, social media, copywriting. Very high volume, lower price per client
4. **Real Estate** — Agents and developers. Need: lead generation, Facebook/Google ads, CRM management. Strong in USA, UAE, Australia
5. **Healthcare & Wellness** — Clinics, practitioners, supplements. Need: local SEO, reputation management, patient lead gen. Regulated but high value

**Daily Task (Tuesday):** Pick one niche. Spend 20 minutes researching it on Google and LinkedIn. Write 5 bullet points: what kind of businesses are in it, what problems they have, what digital marketing services they typically need.

---

**Wednesday — Lesson 1.3: Where Clients Are and Why They're Accessible**

Target markets by country — what you need to know:

- **USA:** Largest market. Clients pay in USD. Expect fast communication, professionalism, results-first culture. Time zones: EST (UTC-5), PST (UTC-8)
- **UK:** Strong market. Clients value quality over quantity. British English only. GMT (UTC+0/+1)
- **Australia:** Fast-growing. Less competition. AEST (UTC+10) — overlap with India morning hours
- **UAE:** High budgets, shorter decision cycles. Arabic + English market. GST (UTC+4)
- **Canada:** Similar to USA, slightly more conservative. EST/PST

Why Indian talent is in demand: cost advantage (same quality, 30–50% lower cost), English fluency, large talent pool, time zone advantage for round-the-clock coverage.

**Daily Task (Wednesday):** Go to LinkedIn and search for "Digital Marketing Manager" in two of the countries above. Look at 5 profiles each. Note: what skills do they list? What companies have they worked for? What does their summary sound like? Add your observations to Notion.

**Thursday — Lesson 1.4: Freelance vs Agency vs In-house**

Three ways to work in digital marketing internationally:

- **Freelance (direct):** You work directly for clients. Full control, higher earnings per hour, but you must find your own clients. Best for self-starters. Income: $500–$5,000/month per client
- **Agency contractor:** You work through a marketing agency that already has clients. Less prospecting, lower cut, but more stable. Income: $300–$2,000/month
- **Remote employment:** Full-time job with an international company. Salary, stability, no client hunting. Competitive to get into, requires proven skills. Income: $800–$3,000/month

**Daily Task (Thursday):** Search Upwork and LinkedIn Jobs for 3 postings in any of the 5 niches. Screenshot them. For each: what is the client asking for? What is the budget? Does your current skill set match? Write a short gap analysis in Notion.

### Week 2: Platforms & Positioning

**Week 2 Learning Goal:** By Friday, you have a clear positioning statement, understand how to price yourself, and have researched what competitors in your niche are offering.

---

**Monday — Lesson 2.1: How to Choose Your Positioning**

Positioning is the answer to: "What do you do, for whom, and with what result?"

- **Generalist:** "I do digital marketing." — Hard to stand out, competes on price, hard to charge premium
- **Specialist:** "I generate B2B leads for SaaS companies using LinkedIn and cold email." — Easy to find, commands higher rates, attracts better clients

How to choose your niche/specialisation:

1. Look at the 5 niches from Week 1 — which one did you find most interesting?
2. Check demand: are there job postings and Upwork projects in that niche?
3. Check competition: are most freelancers offering the same thing, or is there a gap?
4. Make a decision — you can change it in 3 months, but you need to start somewhere

**Daily Task (Monday):** Write a first draft of your positioning statement using this template: "I help [type of client] in [country/region] achieve [result] using [service/method]."

---

**Tuesday — Lesson 2.2: Service Packages — What to Offer, What to Charge**

Service packaging turns your skills into something a client can buy.

Common starter packages:

- **Lead Generation Package** — Apollo list building + LinkedIn outreach + weekly report. Price: $300–$800/month
- **Content & Social Media** — 12 posts/month + captions + scheduling. Price: $200–$600/month
- **Cold Email System** — List building + email copywriting + 30-day sequence setup. Price: $400–$1,000 one-time
- **Full-Service Retainer** — Everything above combined. Price: $800–$2,500/month

Pricing psychology: clients don't buy hours, they buy outcomes. Never say "I charge ₹500/hour" to an international client — say "My monthly retainer for lead generation starts at $500."

**Daily Task (Tuesday):** Design 2 service packages: one entry-level (for when you're a beginner) and one growth-level (for 3 months from now). Give each a name, list what's included, and set a price in USD.

**Monday — Lesson 4.1: ChatGPT & Claude for Professional Output**

AI writing tools are not shortcuts — they are amplifiers. Used correctly, they let you produce professional-quality writing in a fraction of the time.

**The prompting framework (RCTF):**

- **R** — Role: Tell the AI who it is. ("You are a professional B2B copywriter.")
- **C** — Context: Give the background. ("I am a digital marketing freelancer targeting SaaS companies in the USA.")
- **T** — Task: State exactly what you need. ("Write a cold email subject line for a SaaS founder who struggles with lead generation.")
- **F** — Format: Specify the output. ("Give me 5 options, each under 8 words.")

**What to use AI for:**

- Writing and refining cold emails and follow-ups
- Drafting proposals and cover letters
- Generating content ideas and hooks
- Summarising research
- Improving grammar and tone

**What NOT to use AI for (without editing):**

- Sending AI-generated emails directly without personalisation — clients can tell
- Writing case studies or testimonials — must be based on real facts
- Anything that requires your genuine voice and personality

**Daily Task (Monday):** Use ChatGPT or Claude to write 3 subject lines for a cold email to a SaaS company. Use the RCTF framework. Then write 3 more yourself without AI. Compare them — which are better and why? Submit both sets with your analysis.

**Tuesday — Lesson 4.2: Canva for Professional Design**

Canva is used to create media kits, proposals, social content, and presentations. You do not need design experience.

**Media Kit — what it must contain:**

1. **Cover page:** Your name, title (e.g., "Digital Marketing Consultant"), photo, and contact info
2. **About me:** 2–3 sentences — who you help, what you specialise in, where you're based
3. **Services offered:** 2–3 services with a 1-line description each
4. **Portfolio placeholder:** "Case studies and results available on request" OR 1 mock project result
5. **Testimonial placeholder:** "References available from Nivy Academy mentor"
6. **Contact page:** Email, LinkedIn URL, preferred contact method

**Canva setup:**

1. Go to [canva.com](http://canva.com) → sign up free
2. Search for "Media Kit" template → choose a professional, minimal design (avoid overly colourful or playful templates)
3. Colour palette: use a maximum of 2 colours (black + one accent is always professional)
4. Font: stick to 1 font family (Canva's "Montserrat" or "Lato" are safe choices)

**Daily Task (Tuesday):** Start your media kit in Canva. Complete at least the cover page and About section. Save as a draft.

**Wednesday — Lesson 4.3: Loom for Video Communication**

Loom lets you record your screen + face simultaneously. International clients use Loom for walkthroughs, feedback, and check-ins — you must be comfortable with it.

**How to use Loom:**

1. Go to [loom.com](http://loom.com) → sign up free
2. Install the Chrome extension
3. Click "New Recording" → choose "Screen + Camera"
4. Practice recording a 2-minute walkthrough of your Notion workspace

**Professional Loom habits:**

- Always start with: "Hi [Name], [Your Name] here — recording a quick walkthrough of [topic]."
- Keep Looms under 5 minutes unless it's a full proposal walkthrough
- Turn on your camera — face-to-face builds more trust than screen-only
- Use a decent background (plain wall or bookshelf) — not a bed or messy room
- Speak clearly and at a measured pace — not fast

**Thursday — Lesson 4.4: Gmail Professional Setup**

Your email is your primary communication channel. It must look professional.

**Gmail professional setup checklist:**

- [ ]  Email address format: [firstname.lastname@gmail.com](mailto:firstname.lastname@gmail.com) or [firstnamelastname@gmail.com](mailto:firstnamelastname@gmail.com) — no numbers, no nicknames
- [ ]  Email signature set up (Settings → Signature): Name / Title / Email / LinkedIn URL / Optional: "Available for new projects in [niche]"
- [ ]  Grammarly extension installed in Chrome
- [ ]  Read receipts enabled (optional — useful to know if your emails are opened)
- [ ]  Create 3 labels/folders: "Prospects", "Active Clients", "Follow-Ups"

**Professional email rules:**

- Subject line: specific and relevant — never "Hello" or "Enquiry"
- Greeting: "Hi [First Name]," — not "Dear Sir/Madam" or "To Whom It May Concern"
- Body: under 100 words for outreach, under 150 for proposals
- Sign-off: "Best regards," or "Best," — not "Thanks" alone
- Proofread before every send — read it out loud once
