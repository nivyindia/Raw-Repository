# Week 19 — Sales Psychology Fundamentals

## Lesson 19.1 — How International Clients Actually Make Buying Decisions

**Content:** The psychology of trust in B2B sales (people buy from those they trust, then those who can help), the buyer's journey from problem-aware to solution-ready, why price is rarely the real objection, understanding the difference between US, UK, and AU buyer psychology (directness vs relationship-first), the role of social proof in international B2B decisions

**Daily Task:** Read 2 case studies from a successful freelancer or agency (find on LinkedIn or their website). Identify: what created trust, what moved the buyer to act, and what the deciding factor was. Submit a 1-page analysis.

## Lesson 19.2 — The Psychology of Pricing (USD/GBP Mindset)

**Content:** Why charging in Indian rates destroys your international positioning, value-based pricing vs hourly pricing, the anchoring effect in proposals, price bracketing (always present 3 options), why higher prices often win more international clients, handling the internal guilt of charging premium rates

**Daily Task:** Rewrite your service packages from ISP-03 using value-based pricing language. Replace "I will do X" with "You will get Y result from Z." Submit the before and after.

## Lesson 19.3 — The 7 Psychological Triggers in Sales

**Content:** (1) Reciprocity, (2) Social proof, (3) Authority, (4) Liking, (5) Scarcity, (6) Commitment + consistency, (7) Unity. How each trigger is ethically used in international sales conversations, cold outreach, and proposals. Real-world examples for each.

**Daily Task:** Review your cold email templates from ISP-04. Identify which psychological trigger each one uses. Rewrite 2 templates to add a missing trigger. Submit revised versions.

## Lesson 19.4 — Consultative Selling vs Hard Selling

**Content:** Why hard selling destroys relationships with international clients, the consultative approach (ask → listen → understand → advise), becoming a trusted advisor vs a vendor, the difference between a transaction and a relationship, building long-term revenue through consultative sales

**Daily Task:** Role-play exercise. A fictional prospect says: "We're interested but need to think about it." Write 3 different responses: (1) hard sell, (2) passive/no follow-up, (3) consultative. Submit all 3 and explain which is correct.

**Weekly Quiz 19 + Weekly Target:** Revised pricing packages submitted.

# Week 20 — The Discovery Call: Structure & Execution

## Lesson 20.1 — Preparing for a Discovery Call

**Content:** Research the prospect before every call (LinkedIn, website, recent content, Crunchbase, news), creating a pre-call briefing document, your discovery call agenda (shared with client in advance), professional meeting setup (camera, lighting, background, test connection 15 min before), first impression on video calls

**Discovery Call Research SOP:**

1. Google: [Company Name] + [CEO/Decision Maker Name]
2. LinkedIn: check their recent posts and company updates
3. Website: homepage + services + blog + team page
4. Note: recent funding, product launches, hires, or challenges
5. Facebook Ad Library: are they running ads? On what?
6. SEMrush free: traffic trend — going up or down?
7. Write 3 questions specific to what you found
8. Prepare your 60-second intro

**Daily Task:** Research a fictional or real prospect. Build a pre-call briefing document: background, business model, what they’re working on, likely pain points, 3 specific questions to ask. Submit.

## Lesson 20.2 — Discovery Call Structure (The SPIN Framework)

**Content:** SPIN Selling for freelancers: Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-Payoff questions. How to run the first 5 minutes (rapport), how to transition to discovery questions, how to listen more than you talk (70/30 rule), how to take notes without breaking eye contact ([Otter.ai](http://Otter.ai) for recording with permission)

**Discovery Call Script (Full):**

- 0:00–0:02 — Warm welcome, appreciation for their time, brief agenda overview
- 0:02–0:05 — Ask them to share their current situation (Situation questions)
- 0:05–0:12 — Dig into pain points and challenges (Problem questions)
- 0:12–0:18 — Explore impact of those problems on their business (Implication questions)
- 0:18–0:22 — Paint the picture of what solved looks like (Need-Payoff questions)
- 0:22–0:27 — Share your relevant experience / brief case study
- 0:27–0:29 — Explain your process
- 0:29–0:30 — Next steps: proposal timeline, any final questions

**Daily Task:** Practise the discovery call script with a batch-mate or mentor. One plays client, one plays freelancer. Record the call. Self-review using the script structure. Submit recording + 5-point self-assessment.

## Lesson 20.3 — Qualifying Leads on the Call (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline)

**Content:** The BANT qualification framework, how to ask about budget without being awkward ("What’s the investment range you have in mind for this?"), how to confirm you’re speaking with the decision-maker, confirming urgency and timeline, knowing when a prospect is NOT a good fit and how to end the call professionally

**Daily Task:** Write 5 qualifying questions you would ask on a discovery call using BANT. For each, write a follow-up question if the first answer is vague. Submit.

## Lesson 20.4 — Post-Call Follow-Up

**Content:** Send a follow-up email within 2 hours of the call. What to include: summary of what was discussed, what you understood their goals to be, next step (when you will send proposal), something they mentioned you found valuable (shows you listened). Template provided.

**Daily Task:** Write 2 post-call follow-up emails for 2 different fictional call outcomes: (1) strong interest, (2) mildly interested, needs more information. Submit both.

**Weekly Quiz 20 + Weekly Target:** Discovery call practice recording submitted.

# Week 21 — Proposals, Negotiation & Closing

## Lesson 21.1 — Writing a Proposal That Closes

**Content:** The anatomy of a winning proposal: (1) Problem Statement, (2) Recommended Solution, (3) Your Process, (4) Deliverables & Timeline, (5) Investment / Pricing, (6) Testimonials / Social Proof, (7) Next Steps. Tools: Bonsai, PandaDoc, Proposify, or Google Docs. One-page vs multi-page proposals (when to use each). Sending a proposal as a Loom walkthrough video.

**Proposal Template Structure:**

```
Title: [Project Name] — Proposed Engagement
Prepared for: [Client Name], [Company]
Prepared by: [Your Name]
Date: [Date]

Section 1: What We Understand (their problem in their words)
Section 2: Our Recommended Approach (your solution)
Section 3: What You’ll Receive (deliverables, timeline)
Section 4: Our Process (step-by-step, week by week)
Section 5: Investment (3 options: Starter / Growth / Complete)
Section 6: Social Proof (1 testimonial or case study result)
Section 7: Next Steps (sign here, pay here, start date)
```

**Daily Task:** Write a full proposal for a fictional client who needs a 3-month SEO + Content campaign. Use the template. Submit the complete proposal document.

## Lesson 21.2 — Handling Objections

**Content:** The 7 most common international client objections and how to handle each:

1. "It’s too expensive" → reframe around value and ROI
2. "We’re working with someone else" → ask about gaps and results
3. "I need to think about it" → ask what specific thing they need to think through
4. "I don’t have budget right now" → offer a smaller entry point
5. "Can you guarantee results?" → explain what you can and cannot control
6. "You’re in India" → address time zone, communication, work quality directly
7. "Send me your portfolio/case studies first" → send + follow up with a call invite

**Daily Task:** Role-play 3 objection scenarios with a mentor or batch-mate. For each: the objection, your response, their follow-up, your close. Record and submit.

## Lesson 21.3 — Negotiation: Protecting Your Price

**Content:** Never discount — scope down instead (less deliverables for less price), the silence technique after stating your price, how to respond to "can you do it cheaper?" professionally, offering payment plans vs discounts, when to walk away and how to do it gracefully

**Daily Task:** Write responses to these 3 negotiation scenarios: (1) "Can you do $300 instead of $500?", (2) "Can we pay half now and half in 3 months?", (3) "We have another agency quoting half your price." Submit all 3.

## Lesson 21.4 — Closing: Getting the Yes

**Content:** The assumptive close, the alternative close, the summary close, the deadline close. How to ask for the business without desperation. What to do after the verbal yes (contracts, invoice, onboarding trigger). The 48-hour rule (if no response, follow up; if 72 hours, one final reach-out).

**Daily Task:** Write 3 closing messages for 3 different situations: (1) end of a discovery call, (2) follow-up 48 hours after proposal sent, (3) final follow-up 7 days after proposal sent. Submit.

**Weekly Quiz 21 + Weekly Target:** Complete proposal submitted + objection role-plays done.

# 🎭 Discovery Call Role-Play Scenario & Mentor Evaluation Rubric

*(Merged from NA-AC-04, Week 9 — adopted as the standard practical for Phase 5 per the Course Improvement Plan)*

## The Scenario (Read by Mentor — Client Role)

> *"You are the client: I run a SaaS company that helps HR managers automate employee onboarding. We're based in the UK. We have about 15 paying customers and we're trying to get to 50. We've been posting on LinkedIn but it's not working — we get likes but no leads. We tried Google Ads for 2 months and spent £2,000 with zero conversions. I'm not really sure what we should be doing. I've got a budget of around £800/month for marketing. I need to see results within 60 days or my board is going to pull the budget."*
> 

**Scenario context for the mentor:** Play the client naturally — respond only to what the student actually asks. If they skip stages or fail to qualify budget/timeline, let them miss it rather than volunteering the information. Only reveal the budget (£800/month) and the 60-day deadline if the student asks a qualifying question that would logically uncover it.

## Student Task

Run a full 30-minute discovery call using this scenario. Apply:

- The 4-stage structure (Opening → SPIN Discovery → Solution Presentation → Next Steps)
- SPIN questioning framework (Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-Payoff)
- BANT qualification (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline)
- 70/30 listening ratio

Record on Loom. Submit the recording link + a written post-call follow-up email (within the submission, dated as if sent 2 hours after the call).

## Mentor Evaluation Rubric

### Criteria & Scoring

| Criteria | What to Look For | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Opening & Agenda Setting** | Did the student open professionally, confirm time, and set a clear agenda for the call before diving in? | 10 pts |
| **SPIN Questions (minimum 3 before presenting)** | Did they ask at least one question per SPIN stage? Did they let the client finish speaking before moving on? No stacking of multiple questions at once. | 25 pts |
| **70/30 Listening Ratio** | Estimate of talk time: client should have spoken approximately 70% of the call. Student should not be pitching during discovery. | 10 pts |
| **BANT Qualification** | Did they uncover budget (£800/month), confirm decision-making authority, confirm need and urgency (60-day board deadline)? All 4 elements must be addressed. | 15 pts |
| **Solution Presentation (specific, not generic)** | Did the student's proposed solution reference what the client actually said — e.g., LinkedIn not converting, Google Ads failed, 60-day pressure — rather than giving a standard pitch? | 20 pts |
| **Next Steps & Close** | Did they close with a specific, time-bound next step? (e.g., "I'll send a proposal by Thursday" — not just "I'll be in touch") | 10 pts |
| **Post-Call Follow-Up Email** | Sent within 2 hours (notional). Includes: 1-line problem summary, 1-line solution direction, proposal timeline. Professional tone, no spelling errors. | 10 pts |
| **Total** |  | **100 pts** |

**Pass mark:** 70/100 (required to pass the Phase 5 Sales Simulation Assessment)

### Mentor Scoring Notes

- **Opening (10 pts):** 10 = warmly professional, agenda confirmed; 7 = adequate but no agenda; 4 = jumped straight to questions; 0 = no opening
- **SPIN (25 pts):** 25 = all 4 stages used naturally; 20 = 3 stages used; 12 = 2 stages or questions felt scripted; 5 = questions were generic/not SPIN-structured; 0 = pitched immediately
- **Listening (10 pts):** Estimate. 10 = client clearly dominated; 5 = roughly equal; 0 = student dominated
- **BANT (15 pts):** 4 pts per element uncovered (budget, authority, need, timeline) = 16 possible — cap at 15
- **Solution (20 pts):** 20 = every recommendation tied to a client statement; 12 = partially specific; 5 = generic service pitch; 0 = no solution presented
- **Next Steps (10 pts):** 10 = specific action + date; 5 = vague commitment; 0 = no close
- **Email (10 pts):** 10 = all 3 elements + professional; 7 = missing one element; 4 = vague; 0 = not submitted

### Common Failure Patterns (flag these in feedback)

- Pitching before completing discovery (most common)
- Asking stacked questions: "What's your budget and how long has this been going on and what have you tried?"
- Giving a generic proposal (e.g., "I'll do SEO and content") without referencing the £800 budget constraint or 60-day deadline
- Closing with "I'll follow up" rather than committing to a specific deliverable and date
- Skipping BANT qualification entirely — especially budget and timeline

### Resubmission Policy

If the student scores below 70/100: identify the lowest-scoring criteria and require a targeted resubmission within 5 days. Minimum score on resubmission: 65/100.
