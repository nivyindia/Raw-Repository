## Module 3 — The Consultant Mindset (Week 5–6)

### Week 5: Identity & Authority

**Week 5 Learning Goal:** By Friday, you have recorded a professional self-introduction Loom, understand how to communicate with authority, and have begun your Daily Log habit.

---

**Monday — Lesson 5.1: Shifting from Job Seeker to Consultant**

Most students approach clients as if they are applying for a job — they ask "Can I work with you?" instead of "Here is what I see in your business and how I can fix it."

The consultant mindset means:

- You have a specific skill that solves a specific problem
- You are choosing clients as much as they are choosing you
- You do not discount your price to get approval
- You frame conversations around the client's problem, not your need for work

The shift in practice:

- **Job seeker says:** "I am looking for opportunities in digital marketing."
- **Consultant says:** "I work with SaaS companies that are struggling to generate qualified leads. Is that something you're working on?"

The consultant mindset is not arrogance — it is clarity. You know what you do, who you help, and what result you deliver. That confidence is what international clients pay for.

**Daily Task (Monday):** Write 5 "consultant framing" sentences for your niche. Each sentence should position you as someone with a solution, not someone looking for work. Example: "I help e-commerce brands in the US reduce their cost per acquisition by 30% using targeted Facebook and email retargeting."

---

**Tuesday — Lesson 5.2: The Psychology of Authority**

Clients do not hire the cheapest option — they hire the person who feels most authoritative and trustworthy.

How to build authority without a large portfolio:

1. **Specificity signals expertise.** "I specialise in LinkedIn outreach for B2B SaaS" sounds more credible than "I do social media."
2. **Language signals expertise.** Use industry terms correctly: CAC, LTV, CTR, MQL, SQLs, churn, retargeting. Learn what they mean and use them naturally.
3. **Process signals expertise.** "My 3-step outreach system" sounds more credible than "I send emails."
4. **Data signals expertise.** Even benchmarks work: "Most B2B cold email campaigns get a 2–5% reply rate. My sequences average 8%." (You can legitimately claim Nivy Academy benchmark data once you've run a real campaign.)
5. **Proof signals expertise.** Your Opportunity Report, prospect list, and media kit from Month 1 are your first proof assets.

**Daily Task (Tuesday):** Rewrite your personal brand statement from Month 1 using authority language. Add: (a) a specific process name, (b) one industry term used correctly, (c) a benchmark or result claim.

**Wednesday — Lesson 5.3: Setting Boundaries with Clients from Day 1**

Scope creep (clients asking for more than what they paid for) and communication overload (clients messaging at all hours) are the two most common reasons freelancers burn out or lose clients.

Setting boundaries professionally:

- **Scope:** Define deliverables in writing before starting. "This retainer includes X, Y, and Z. Any additional services will be scoped and priced separately."
- **Communication hours:** "My working hours are Monday–Friday, 10 AM–7 PM IST. I respond to messages within 24 hours on business days."
- **Revision limits:** "Each deliverable includes 2 rounds of revisions. Additional revisions are billed at [rate]."
- **Meeting limits:** "Our retainer includes one 30-minute check-in call per month."

These are not rules you announce on day one unprompted — you state them in your onboarding email and proposal. Professional clients expect them and respect them.

**Daily Task (Wednesday):** Write your personal "Client Standards Statement" — a short paragraph (80–100 words) that you will paste into every client onboarding email. It should cover: your communication hours, response time, revision policy, and scope policy.

---

**Thursday — Lesson 5.4: Handling Imposter Syndrome**

Imposter syndrome is the feeling that you are not qualified, not experienced enough, or that you will be "found out." Every new freelancer experiences it. Here is how to handle it:

Facts that counteract it:

- Your prospect has a real problem. Your solution — even if imperfect — is better than them doing nothing.
- International clients are not looking for perfection; they are looking for progress, communication, and reliability.
- You have spent a full month learning tools and processes that most of their local hires have never studied.
- Confidence is built through action, not waiting until you "feel ready."

The 3-part reset when imposter syndrome hits:

1. Go back to your Opportunity Report and prospect list — you did real research, you identified real gaps
2. Read your personal brand statement out loud three times
3. Do one outreach action (send one message, connect with one prospect) — action is the antidote

**Daily Task (Thursday):** Write a short "evidence log" — a list of 10 things you have actually learned, built, or completed since starting Month 1. Read it back to yourself. This is your running evidence that you are qualified to do this work.

**Friday — Lesson 5.5: International Communication Standards + Week 5 Task**

When working with international clients, communication is as important as the work itself.

Core standards:

- **Tone:** Professional but warm — not stiff, not casual. "Hi Sarah, hope you're having a great week! Here's the update you asked for." is correct. "Hey" is too casual. "Dear Ms. Smith" is too formal for digital marketing.
- **Grammar and spelling:** No typos. Ever. Install Grammarly on Chrome. Read every message out loud before sending.
- **Response time:** Respond within 24 hours on business days. If you cannot respond fully, acknowledge: "Hi [Name], got your message — I'll send a full response by tomorrow morning."
- **Punctuation:** Do not use exclamation marks more than once per email. "Great!!!" looks unprofessional.
- **Abbreviations:** Never use "u", "r", "gonna", "wanna", "lol" with clients.

### Week 6: Proposal & Pitch Mastery

**Week 6 Learning Goal:** By Friday, you can write a professional cold email, understand the proposal structure, and have 3 cold email variants written and reviewed.

---

**Monday — Lesson 6.1: The Anatomy of a Cold Email That Gets Replies**

Cold email is not spam — it is targeted, personalised, professional outreach.

**The AIDA-P framework for cold email:**

- **A — Attention:** First line must hook. Reference something specific about their business.
- **I — Interest:** Show you understand their problem. One sentence.
- **D — Desire:** Show your solution and the result it delivers. One to two sentences.
- **A — Action:** One clear CTA. Not "Let me know if you're interested" — be specific.
- **P — Personalisation:** At least one line that could ONLY be written to this specific person.

**Example cold email (annotated):**

> Subject: Your Shopify store's abandoned cart rate — quick idea
> 

> 
> 

> Hi [Name],
> 

> 
> 

> [Attention] I came across [Company] while researching e-commerce brands in the home décor space — noticed you're running Google Ads but don't appear to have an abandoned cart email sequence set up.
> 

> 
> 

> [Interest + Desire] Most Shopify stores lose 60–70% of potential revenue from abandoned carts. A 3-email recovery sequence typically recovers 10–15% of those — that's often $5,000–$20,000/month in revenue that's currently being left behind.
> 

> 
> 

> [Action + Personalisation] I specialise in email sequences for Shopify brands. Would it make sense to jump on a 15-minute call this week to see if there's a fit?
> 

> 
> 

> Best,
> 

> [Your Name]
> 

Total: 97 words. Personalised. Specific. Clear CTA.

**Tuesday — Lesson 6.2: LinkedIn Outreach Strategy**

LinkedIn cold outreach has two channels: connection requests and InMail.

**Connection request (free):**

- Send with a personalised note (300 character limit)
- Template: "Hi [Name], I came across your profile while researching [industry] companies in [country]. I specialise in [service] for [type of company] — thought it was worth connecting. [Your Name]"
- Do not pitch in the connection request — just connect
- After they accept: wait 24–48 hours, then send a message with your value proposition

**InMail (paid, LinkedIn Premium):**

- Longer message (up to 1,900 characters), sent to anyone without needing to connect first
- Use same AIDA-P structure as cold email
- InMail gets 3x higher open rates than cold email on average

**The LinkedIn sequence (free version):**

1. Day 1: Send connection request with personalised note
2. Day 3 (after acceptance): Send value message (AIDA-P, under 150 words)
3. Day 7 (no reply): Send gentle follow-up — "Hi [Name], just following up on my message. Happy to share more context or answer any questions."
4. Day 14 (no reply): Final follow-up — "Hi [Name], I'll leave it here — happy to reconnect if the timing is better in future. Best, [Name]"

**Wednesday — Lesson 6.3: Pricing Yourself Confidently**

Most beginners underprice because they are afraid of rejection. Here is how to price with confidence.

**Pricing anchors:**

- Hourly: $15–$40/hour (beginner) → $40–$100/hour (experienced)
- Monthly retainer: $300–$800/month (starter package) → $800–$3,000/month (full-service)
- Project: $200–$500 (one-time audit or setup) → $1,000–$5,000 (full campaign launch)

**The confidence script when a client asks "What do you charge?"**

"My monthly retainer for [specific service] starts at $[price]. That covers [deliverables]. The exact scope depends on your business — can I ask a couple of questions first to give you a more accurate figure?"

This moves the conversation to discovery before the client anchors on price.

**When to discount:** Almost never in the first conversation. If a client pushes back on price, offer a reduced-scope starter project: "If budget is a concern right now, I offer a one-month trial package at $[lower price] covering [fewer deliverables]. That gives us both a chance to see the results before committing to a full retainer."

**Daily Task (Wednesday):** Write out your pricing for 2 packages (from Week 2) and practise saying them out loud five times. Then write the confidence script in your own words. Submit the script.

**Thursday — Lesson 6.4: The Discovery Call Framework**

The discovery call is not a sales pitch — it is a diagnostic conversation. Your job is to understand the client's problem deeply enough to know if you can solve it.

**The SPIN Framework (adapted for digital marketing services):**

- **S — Situation:** "Can you tell me a bit about your business and how you're currently getting clients?"
- **P — Problem:** "What's been the biggest challenge with [lead gen / marketing / outreach] for you?"
- **I — Implication:** "How is that affecting your growth? Revenue? Team time?"
- **N — Need-payoff:** "If we could solve [that specific problem], what would that be worth to you?"

After SPIN, you present your solution: "Based on what you've told me, I think [specific service] would address [specific problem]. Here's how I'd approach it..."

Then: "Does that sound like what you're looking for, or is there something I've missed?"

Closing the discovery call:

- If fit: "I'll send you a proposal within 48 hours. Does that work?"
- If not fit: "Based on what you've described, I don't think I'm the best fit for this — but I'd be happy to refer you to someone who specialises in [their actual need]."

**Daily Task (Thursday):** Write out your 5 SPIN questions adapted for your specific niche. Then write the "solution presentation" paragraph you would say after completing SPIN with a client in your niche.

**Friday — Lesson 6.5: Following Up Without Being Annoying + Week 6 Task**

The fortune is in the follow-up. Most deals are closed on the 4th–7th touchpoint, but most freelancers give up after 1–2.

**The professional follow-up sequence:**

- **Day 0:** Send initial outreach
- **Day 3:** "Hi [Name], just following up on my email — happy to answer any questions or share more examples."
- **Day 7:** "Hi [Name], I know things get busy — sending one more note in case this got buried. If the timing isn't right, no worries at all."
- **Day 14:** Final: "Hi [Name], I'll stop following up after this — but if [specific pain point] ever becomes a priority, I'm happy to reconnect. Best of luck with [something specific about their business]."

After 4 touchpoints with no reply: mark as "No Response — Archived" in your CRM. Move on.

**Monday — Lesson 7.1: Setting Up Apollo Sequences**

An Apollo sequence is an automated multi-step email outreach campaign. You write the emails once, set the timing, and Apollo sends them automatically.

**Step-by-step — Building an Apollo sequence:**

1. Log into Apollo → "Sequences" → "New Sequence"
2. Name it: "[Your Niche] — Outreach Sprint Week 7"
3. Add Step 1: Email → Paste your best cold email (100 words, AIDA-P) → Set delay: Day 0 (immediate)
4. Add Step 2: Email → Follow-up message (50 words, "just following up") → Set delay: Day 3
5. Add Step 3: Email → Final follow-up (40 words, graceful exit) → Set delay: Day 7
6. Click "Add People" → select your prospect list from Week 3 → "Add to Sequence"
7. Review settings: "Stop sequence when prospect replies" must be ON
8. Launch sequence → monitor daily

**Daily outreach target from today:** Minimum 20 new contacts added to sequence per day.

**Wednesday — Lesson 7.3: WhatsApp Outreach for Regional Prospects**

WhatsApp outreach is appropriate for Indian, Middle Eastern, and some Southeast Asian prospects — not for USA/UK/Australia where it is considered intrusive without permission.

**When to use WhatsApp:**

- You have found a local business or regional prospect whose WhatsApp number is publicly listed
- You have already connected with them on LinkedIn or email and they suggested WhatsApp
- The prospect is in India, UAE, or another WhatsApp-dominant market

**WhatsApp outreach rules:**

- Never send voice notes as a first message
- Keep initial messages under 3 lines
- Always introduce yourself by name and context: "Hi [Name], I'm [Your Name], a digital marketing consultant. I saw your business [X] while researching [niche] companies in [city]. Would it be ok to share a brief note on how I could help with [specific thing]?"
- Do not follow up on WhatsApp more than twice without a reply

**Thursday — Lesson 7.4: What to Do When Someone Replies**

A reply — even a "not interested" — is a live lead. How you respond determines everything.

**Positive reply ("Tell me more" / "Let's chat"):**

1. Reply within 4 hours (ideally within 1 hour)
2. Thank them briefly — one line
3. Ask for their preferred call time: "What times work best for you this week for a 15-minute call? I'm available [give 3 specific slots in their time zone]"
4. Send a calendar invite once time is agreed (use Calendly free tier)
5. Log as "Meeting Booked" in CRM

**Neutral reply ("Not right now" / "Maybe later"):**

1. Respond warmly: "No worries at all — I'll follow up in [30/60] days if that's ok with you?"
2. Log as "Nurture" in CRM — set a reminder to follow up at the stated date

**Negative reply ("Not interested" / "Remove me"):**

1. Reply: "Of course — thanks for taking the time to reply. Best of luck with [their business]!"
2. Log as "Not Interested — Closed" in CRM
3. Do not follow up again

**Monday — Lesson 8.1: Reading Your Outreach Data**

After one week of live outreach, you have real data. Now you need to read it.

**Key metrics:**

- **Emails sent:** Total messages launched in Apollo
- **Open rate:** % of recipients who opened your email. Industry average: 20–30%. Below 15% = subject line problem
- **Reply rate:** % of recipients who replied to any email. Industry average: 2–5%. Above 8% = excellent
- **Positive reply rate:** % of replies that expressed interest. Target: 30–50% of replies should be positive
- **Connection acceptance rate (LinkedIn):** % of requests accepted. Average: 20–35%. Below 15% = profile or note problem

**Where to find the data:**

- Apollo: Sequence dashboard → click your sequence → "Analytics" tab
- LinkedIn: Manual count from your CRM
- Google Sheets: Use a formula to calculate rates: =COUNTIF(Status column,"Replied")/COUNTIF(Status column,"Contacted")

**Tuesday — Lesson 8.2: A/B Testing Your Outreach**

A/B testing means sending two different versions of a message to different groups to see which performs better.

**What to test in cold email:**

- Subject line A vs B (most important — affects open rate)
- Opening line A vs B (affects reply rate)
- CTA A vs B (affects meeting booking rate)

**How to run a simple A/B test in Apollo:**

1. Duplicate your sequence → rename "Variant B"
2. Change only one variable (e.g., the subject line)
3. Split your contact list: send 50 contacts to Variant A, 50 to Variant B
4. After 7 days: compare open rates → keep the better subject line going forward

**Rule:** Only change ONE variable at a time. If you change both the subject line and the opening, you cannot know which change caused the improvement.

**Wednesday — Lesson 8.3: Pivoting Based on Results**

Data tells you the truth. Here is how to respond to what you find:

| Problem | Likely Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open rate below 15% | Subject line is weak or generic | Test 2 new subject lines |
| Reply rate below 2% | Email body too generic or too long | Shorten to 80 words, add more personalisation |
| Positive reply rate below 20% | Wrong targeting or wrong offer | Review prospect list niche/title filters |
| LinkedIn acceptance below 15% | Profile incomplete or note too salesy | Update LinkedIn headline and rewrite connection note |
| Zero WhatsApp replies | Wrong market or too aggressive opener | Stop WhatsApp for this niche; focus on email/LinkedIn |

**Do not pivot your entire niche based on 1 week of data.** Wait until you have sent at least 100 contacts before making niche changes. Messaging can almost always be fixed faster than niche switching.
