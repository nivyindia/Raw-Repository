# International Business Setup: Choosing and Structuring Your Entity

Most founders treat entity choice as a legal and administrative question — which form to file, which registered agent to use, how fast incorporation happens. It's actually a tax decision wearing legal clothing, and the structure you choose at the start determines how every dollar of profit gets taxed, repatriated, and reported for as long as the entity exists. Here's how to think about it across the jurisdictions we work in most.

## Why Entity Choice Is a Tax Decision, Not Just a Legal One

Incorporation paperwork asks you to pick a legal form — LLC, Ltd, Corp, Pte Ltd. But underneath that legal label, each jurisdiction's tax authority applies its own classification: is this entity **opaque** (taxed as its own separate taxpayer) or **transparent** (income flows straight through to the owner's personal return)? That classification, not the legal label, determines how profit is taxed, when it's taxed, and — critically for cross-border structures — whether two different countries agree on the classification at all.

When they don't agree, you get a **classification mismatch**: the same entity treated as transparent by one tax authority and opaque by another. That mismatch is where most of the expensive cross-border structuring mistakes live, and it's entirely avoidable if it's identified before incorporation rather than discovered at tax time.

## Jurisdiction Comparison

| Entity | Ownership | Tax Treatment | Typical Use Case |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **US LLC / C-Corp** | LLC: any; C-Corp: unlimited shareholders, any nationality | LLC: disregarded/partnership by default; C-Corp: separate taxpayer (Form 1120) | C-Corp preferred for foreign investors needing clean entity separation; LLC common for US-only founders |
| **UK Ltd** | Any nationality, no local director requirement | Opaque — Corporation Tax on profits, dividends taxed separately | International groups wanting a structure almost universally recognized as opaque |
| **Canadian Corp (CCPC or federal)** | CCPC requires Canadian-resident control to keep status | T2 corporate return; CCPCs get Small Business Deduction on first CAD $500K active income | Canadian-controlled operating businesses; federal incorporation for multi-province plans |
| **UAE Free Zone / Mainland** | 100% foreign ownership available in both as standard | Free Zone: 0% on qualifying income if QFZP conditions met, 9% otherwise; Mainland: 9% above AED 375,000 | Free Zone for export-focused/holding structures; Mainland for direct UAE-market trade |
| **Singapore Pte Ltd** | 100% foreign ownership permitted | Territorial — foreign-sourced income generally untaxed unless remitted | Regional and group holding structures, leveraging the 90+ country treaty network |

## The Free Zone vs. Mainland Decision in Depth

For businesses considering the UAE specifically, this is usually the first and most consequential decision. The short version: Free Zone entities can access a 0% Corporate Tax rate on qualifying income, but only by meeting the Qualifying Free Zone Person conditions — adequate UAE substance, income limited to qualifying activities, non-qualifying income kept below a de minimis threshold. Fail any one of these and the *entire* income, not just the excess, can lose 0% eligibility. Mainland entities skip that complexity at the cost of a flat 9% rate above AED 375,000, but gain unrestricted access to the UAE domestic market that Free Zone entities don't have without a local distributor or branch.

The deciding factor is almost always where your customers actually are, not which option is cheaper to set up. Export-focused, holding, and consultancy structures tend to fit Free Zone well; anything selling directly to UAE consumers or requiring physical on-the-ground presence tends to fit Mainland better. See the full breakdown, including the QFZP conditions in detail, in our dedicated article below.

## Multi-Currency Operations: The Part Nobody Plans For

Entity structuring decisions are usually made with tax treatment front of mind — multi-currency bookkeeping is rarely part of the conversation until it becomes a problem. But the moment your structure spans more than one currency — USD revenue with GBP costs, a UAE entity invoicing in AED while paying a Singapore holding company in SGD — the bookkeeping behind that structure carries real exposure of its own.

Multi-currency accounting software converts transactions automatically, but it doesn't catch the judgment calls: a marketplace payout that bundles sales, fees, and refunds in different currencies; a foreign currency balance that needs month-end revaluation; a transaction that should have used a contracted rate instead of the spot rate the software defaulted to. None of these show up as software errors — they show up as numbers that are quietly wrong until someone files a tax return on top of them. A structure decision that ignores this ends up costing more in cleanup than it saved in tax efficiency.

## Common Structuring Mistakes

Across every jurisdiction in this guide, the same handful of mistakes recur:

**The LLC disregarded-entity trap.** A US LLC owned by a UK or Canadian resident is disregarded by the IRS by default — but the UK and Canada generally treat that same LLC as an opaque corporation. The result is a classification mismatch where the same income gets characterized two different ways by two tax authorities simultaneously, and foreign tax credits don't always line up cleanly across that gap.

**Missing substance requirements.** Singapore holding structures and UAE Free Zone entities both depend on demonstrating genuine management substance — real decisions made where the entity is registered — not just a registered address. Substance is assessed on an ongoing basis; a structure that was sound at incorporation can drift out of compliance as the business changes.

**Treating incorporation as a one-time decision.** A Free Zone entity's QFZP eligibility, a CCPC's foreign-control status, and a holding company's substance position all need reviewing as the business evolves — not just confirmed once at setup and forgotten.
