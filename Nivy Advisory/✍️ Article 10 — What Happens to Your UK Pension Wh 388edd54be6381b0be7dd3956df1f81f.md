# What Happens to Your UK Pension When You Move Abroad?

A UK pension doesn't stop existing just because you've left the UK — but what happens to it next depends heavily on the type of pension, where you're moving to, and choices that, once made, are often irreversible. This is the mirror image of the 401(k) question for US expats, and it carries its own distinct set of rules.

## The Two Starting Points: Leave It, or Transfer It

Most UK pension holders moving abroad face a binary choice that shapes everything else: leave the pension invested in the UK and draw from it later, or transfer it to a scheme recognized in the new country of residence. Neither is automatically better — the right answer depends on the specific schemes involved, the destination country, and how the income will eventually be taxed.

## Leaving It in the UK

A UK pension left in place continues to be governed by UK pension rules regardless of where you live:

- **Tax-free growth continues** under UK rules while invested
- **Withdrawals are generally still taxable in the UK** as pension income, though the **UK-host country tax treaty** may determine which country has primary taxing rights once you're a confirmed non-resident — some treaties allocate pension income to the country of residence instead
- **The 25% UK tax-free lump sum** (Pension Commencement Lump Sum) is a UK-specific concept; some destination countries don't recognize it as tax-free under their own rules, meaning the same lump sum can be untaxed in the UK and taxed locally

## Transferring to a Recognized Overseas Pension Scheme

Transferring is possible via a **Qualifying Recognised Overseas Pension Scheme (QROPS)** — but this is a decision with real, sometimes irreversible, consequences:

- **The Overseas Transfer Charge** can apply (currently 25% in many cases) unless specific conditions are met — broadly, where you and the receiving scheme are both genuinely in the same country, among other conditions that change periodically
- **Not every overseas scheme qualifies** as a QROPS — transferring to a non-qualifying scheme can trigger an unauthorized payment charge of up to 55% of the transferred value
- **Once transferred, UK pension protections** (certain guarantees, in some cases UK regulatory oversight) generally don't follow the funds — you're now governed by the receiving country's pension and tax rules

The charge and qualification rules around QROPS transfers change periodically, which is exactly why this isn't a decision to make from a general guide alone — the specific rules in effect at the time of transfer matter enormously.

## SIPPs Specifically: A Common Source of US Complication

For UK nationals moving specifically to the **United States**, a Self-Invested Personal Pension (SIPP) carries additional complexity covered in more depth in our US-UK treaty article: a SIPP can, depending on how it's structured, trigger US reporting obligations under rules for foreign trusts, and the timing mismatch between UK tax-free growth and US taxation of that same growth is a frequent source of unplanned US tax exposure for UK nationals who relocate to the US without addressing their SIPP position first.

## What Doesn't Change Regardless of Where You Move

- **UK State Pension** entitlement is based on National Insurance contribution history and generally isn't affected by where you live, though the **annual increases** to your State Pension amount may or may not apply depending on whether your destination country has a reciprocal social security agreement with the UK
- **Reporting obligations** — depending on your new country of residence, you may have local reporting requirements for foreign pension assets that exist independently of whatever the UK requires

## What This Means in Practice

The leave-it-or-transfer-it decision should be made by working backward from your destination country's tax treatment of foreign pensions and the specific UK-host country tax treaty provisions on pension income — not by defaulting to whichever option sounds simpler. A transfer that avoids UK complexity can create host-country complexity instead, and the Overseas Transfer Charge rules mean getting the timing or scheme choice wrong can be a expensive, one-way decision.
