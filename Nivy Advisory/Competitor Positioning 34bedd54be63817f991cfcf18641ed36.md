# Competitor Positioning

> How to respond when prospects compare Nivy Tax to alternatives. This is not about attacking competitors — it's about clarifying our unique value.
> 

---

## Common Competitor Types We Face

### 1. Local CPA Firms (US-Based)

**What they offer:** US tax expertise, local credibility

**Their weakness:** High cost ($300–$500/hr), limited international reach, slow response times

**Our response:** *"We offer the same technical expertise with a dedicated team model, faster turnaround, and significant cost savings — plus we can handle your cross-border needs as you grow."*

### 2. Freelance Bookkeepers / Accountants

**What they offer:** Low cost, flexibility

**Their weakness:** Single point of failure, limited expertise, no strategic advisory

**Our response:** *"With a freelancer, your finances depend entirely on one person. With us, you get a full team — accountant, tax specialist, and account manager — all coordinated for you."*

### 3. DIY Software (QuickBooks, FreshBooks)

**What they offer:** Low cost, control

**Their weakness:** Time-consuming, error-prone, no advisory layer

**Our response:** *"Most business owners who switch to us were using software but found they weren't doing it correctly — or were spending 8–10 hours a month on it. We handle it and give you that time back."*

### 4. Big 4 / Large Accounting Firms

**What they offer:** Brand prestige, full service

**Their weakness:** Very expensive, clients get junior staff, slow, impersonal

**Our response:** *"We're the better fit for growing businesses — you get senior attention without enterprise-level fees."*

---

## The Universal Positioning Move

When asked *"Why not just use [X]?"* — always acknowledge the competitor's strength first, then pivot to what we do differently:

> *"[Competitor] is a great option for [their strength]. Where we differ is [our USP]. For a business like yours that [client's situation], that matters because [specific reason]."*
