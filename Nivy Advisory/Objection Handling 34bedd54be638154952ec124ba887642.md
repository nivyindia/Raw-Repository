# Objection Handling

> The most common pricing and sales objections, and how to respond professionally and effectively.
> 

---

## The 5 Most Common Objections

### Objection 1: "It's too expensive."

**Don't:** Discount immediately or get defensive.

**Do:** Reframe around value.

> *"I understand — let me ask: what are you currently spending on accounting, and how many hours a month are you or your team spending on it? Most clients find that when they add up the time cost and the errors they're catching, the investment pays for itself quickly."*
> 

---

### Objection 2: "I'll think about it."

**Don't:** Say "sure, take your time" and disappear.

**Do:** Understand the real hesitation.

> *"Of course — is there a specific part you'd like to think through? Sometimes there's a question I haven't answered, or a concern I can address right now."*
> 

---

### Objection 3: "My cousin / friend does my accounting."

**Don't:** Attack the cousin or friend.

**Do:** Acknowledge and redirect.

> *"That's great that you have someone you trust. A lot of our clients started the same way. What usually brings them to us is when things get more complex — multi-state taxes, investor reporting, or just more volume. Is there anything like that happening in your business?"*
> 

---

### Objection 4: "I just use QuickBooks / software."

**Don't:** Dismiss the software.

**Do:** Acknowledge and pivot.

> *"QuickBooks is a great tool — and we actually use it ourselves. The difference is having a qualified professional manage it, so the data going in is accurate and the insights coming out are actually useful. Most people using it themselves find they're not sure if they're doing it right."*
> 

---

### Objection 5: "Can you give me a discount?"

**Don't:** Discount without a reason.

**Do:** Anchor to value, then offer an alternative.

> *"Our pricing reflects the level of service and quality we deliver consistently. What I can do is [suggest a lower package if appropriate / offer a payment plan / provide a one-month trial at reduced scope]. Would either of those work for you?"*
>
