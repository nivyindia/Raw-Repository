# Basic Accounting Concepts

> Every VA must understand these concepts before selling accounting services. You don't need to be an accountant — but you need to speak the language fluently.
> 

---

## The 5 Concepts Every VA Must Know

### 1. Revenue vs. Profit

- **Revenue (Turnover):** Total money coming into the business before any expenses
- **Profit:** What's left after all expenses are paid
- **Why it matters:** Clients often confuse the two. A business can have high revenue and still lose money.

> *"Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity, cash is reality."*
> 

### 2. Cash Flow vs. Profit

- **Profit** is an accounting number (can be on paper)
- **Cash flow** is actual money available in the bank
- A profitable business can still go broke if cash doesn't come in fast enough
- **Why it matters:** Many prospects' biggest fear is running out of cash — connect this to our reporting services

### 3. The Chart of Accounts

- Every transaction is coded to a category (rent, salaries, revenue, etc.)
- This system is called the **Chart of Accounts**
- Good categorisation = accurate reports
- **Why it matters:** Explains why bookkeeping is not just "data entry" — accuracy requires expertise

### 4. Reconciliation

- Matching the books to actual bank statements
- Catches errors, fraud, and missing transactions
- **Why it matters:** If books aren't reconciled, nothing can be trusted — not the P&L, not the tax return

### 5. Tax vs. Accounting (Two Different Things)

- **Bookkeeping / Accounting:** Recording what happened financially
- **Tax Filing:** Reporting it to the government in the required format
- Businesses need both — they're not the same thing
- **Why it matters:** Many prospects think they only need one — this helps explain the full picture

## Glossary of Key Terms

| Term | Plain-English Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| P&L / Income Statement | Revenue minus expenses = profit or loss |
| Balance Sheet | Snapshot: what you own, what you owe, what's yours |
| Accounts Receivable (AR) | Money clients owe you |
| Accounts Payable (AP) | Money you owe suppliers |
| Accrual | Recording income/expenses when earned, not when paid |
| Cash Basis | Recording when money actually moves |
| Reconciliation | Matching books to bank statements |
| Depreciation | Spreading the cost of an asset over its useful life |
| EIN | Employer Identification Number (US tax ID for businesses) |
| 1099 | US tax form for contractor payments |
| W-2 | US tax form for employee wages |
