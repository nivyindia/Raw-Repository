# Free Zone or Mainland? Choosing a UAE Entity Structure

"Just set up in a Free Zone" is the advice almost every founder gets before incorporating in the UAE — and for a meaningful share of businesses, it's right. But it's not right for all of them, and the cases where it's wrong tend to be expensive to unwind later. Here's how to actually make the call, rather than defaulting to the version that sounds simpler.

## What Free Zone and Mainland Actually Mean in Practice

The UAE has two parallel licensing regimes for businesses. A **Free Zone** entity is set up within one of the UAE's many designated economic zones (each with its own regulator), historically built to attract foreign investment with 100% foreign ownership and streamlined setup. A **Mainland** entity is licensed by the Department of Economic Development in the relevant emirate and can trade anywhere in the UAE without restriction.

The ownership distinction that used to separate them — Free Zones allowing 100% foreign ownership versus Mainland requiring a local sponsor — has largely closed since 2021 reforms opened most Mainland activities to full foreign ownership too. What hasn't closed is the difference in **where you're allowed to operate** and **how Corporate Tax applies to you**.

## Ownership and Operating Restrictions Compared

| Factor | Free Zone | Mainland |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ownership** | 100% foreign ownership as standard | 100% foreign ownership permitted for most activities (post-2021 reform) |
| **Where you can trade** | Restricted from direct trade with the UAE mainland without a local distributor or branch | Unrestricted access to the UAE mainland market |
| **Office requirement** | Flexi-desk or shared office options widely available, often lower cost | Physical office typically required; higher setup overhead |
| **Visa allocation** | Tied to office package size (e.g. flexi-desk = limited visas) | Generally more flexible, tied to office size and licensed activity |

The trading restriction is the one that catches people most often. A Free Zone company set up cheaply and quickly can find itself legally unable to sell directly to UAE-based customers without routing the transaction through a local distributor or setting up a Mainland branch — which defeats a lot of the cost advantage that drew them to a Free Zone in the first place.

## Corporate Tax Treatment Differences

This is where the decision has the most financial weight, and where "Free Zone is automatically cheaper" stops being reliably true.

Mainland entities pay UAE Corporate Tax at **9% on profits above AED 375,000**, full stop. Free Zone entities can qualify for a **0% rate on "qualifying income"** — but only if they meet the **Qualifying Free Zone Person (QFZP)** conditions, which require:

- Maintaining **adequate substance** in the UAE (real operations, not just a registered address)
- Deriving income only from defined **qualifying activities**
- Keeping non-qualifying income below a **de minimis threshold**

The part that surprises businesses who set up expecting an automatic 0% rate: failing any one of these conditions doesn't just tax the excess income at 9% — in many cases it disqualifies the entity's *entire* income from the 0% rate for that period. This is a binary eligibility test, not a sliding scale, and it's reviewed on an ongoing basis, not just confirmed once at incorporation.

## When Mainland Makes More Sense Despite Free Zone's Reputation

Free Zone structures are well-suited to export-focused businesses, holding companies, e-commerce operations selling outside the UAE, and consultancies serving clients abroad — anywhere the qualifying-income conditions are straightforward to meet because the customer base is already outside the UAE.

Mainland makes more sense once any of the following is true:

- You're selling directly to UAE consumers, retail customers, or government entities
- Your business is F&B, on-the-ground services, or anything requiring physical presence and unrestricted local trade
- You expect UAE-sourced revenue to grow past the de minimis threshold for a Free Zone entity, putting QFZP status at risk
- The administrative cost of routing mainland sales through a distributor or branch would exceed the tax savings from the 0% rate

In other words: the right structure follows where your customers and operations actually sit — not which option sounded easier to set up on day one.

## What This Means in Practice

The decision isn't "Free Zone vs. Mainland" as a one-time choice at incorporation — it's an ongoing qualifying-income position that needs reviewing as your customer base and revenue mix evolve. A Free Zone entity that qualified for 0% in year one can lose that status in year three if UAE-sourced revenue grows past the de minimis line, and the fix (restructuring, adding a Mainland branch) is far cheaper to plan for in advance than to react to after an assessment.
