## 3 Things UK Cross-Border Filers Get Wrong

**1. "I've left the UK, so I no longer have UK tax obligations."**

Leaving the UK does not automatically end your UK tax liability. The Statutory Residence Test (SRT) is a complex day-count and tie-breaker test. Rental income from a UK property, UK pension income, and UK-sourced dividends remain taxable in the UK regardless of where you live. Getting residency status wrong creates years of backdated liability.

**2. "My company's year-end is 31 December, so my CT600 is due 31 December the following year."**

Not quite — the CT600 is due 12 months after the company's accounting period end, but the Corporation Tax payment is due 9 months and 1 day after. Most small company directors miss this distinction and pay late. Late CT payment carries interest from HMRC from the first day.

**3. "I don't need to register for VAT yet — my turnover is below the threshold."**

The UK VAT registration threshold (currently £90,000 for 2024/25) applies to UK-sourced turnover. However, if you're a non-UK business selling digital services to UK consumers, different rules apply — you may have obligations regardless of turnover. Post-Brexit import VAT rules also catch overseas sellers by surprise.

## UK Tax Treaties — What They Mean for You

The UK has comprehensive double tax treaties with over 130 countries, including the US, Canada, Australia, UAE, and Singapore. These treaties:

- Determine which country has primary taxing rights on specific income types (salary, dividends, royalties, pensions)
- Prevent the same income being taxed in full by both countries
- Set the rules for tax residence tie-breakers when you qualify under both countries' domestic tests

**The UK-US treaty** is particularly complex for US citizens in the UK — it interacts with the US's citizenship-based taxation in ways that require specific elections and treaty position filings on the US side. This is a coordination problem, not something one-country advisors can resolve.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to file a Self Assessment return if I'm employed and pay tax through PAYE?**

Not always — but if you have additional income (rental, freelance, overseas, investments over £10,000), have untaxed income, or are a higher-rate taxpayer with certain reliefs to claim, HMRC requires a Self Assessment return. We assess whether you're required to file as part of onboarding.

**I'm not a UK resident but I receive UK rental income. What are my obligations?**

You're liable to UK Income Tax on UK rental income regardless of where you live. You should register under the Non-Resident Landlord (NRL) scheme to receive your income gross (otherwise the letting agent or tenant must withhold 20% tax). You'll also need to file a Self Assessment return annually.

**What does Making Tax Digital mean for my business?**

MTD for VAT is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses — you must keep digital records and submit returns using MTD-compatible software. MTD for Income Tax is being phased in from April 2026 for self-employed individuals and landlords above the earnings threshold. We handle all MTD submissions as part of our bookkeeping and tax service.

**Can you handle both my UK Corporation Tax and my US tax obligations?**

Yes — and this is the scenario where a coordinated engagement delivers the most value. UK-US coordination involves treaty elections, dividend timing, and residency position that need to be optimised together. We handle both sides under one engagement.

**What's IR35 and how do I know if it applies to me?**

IR35 (off-payroll working rules) determines whether a contractor working through a limited company should be treated as an employee for tax purposes. Since 2021, medium/large UK businesses are responsible for making this determination for contractors they engage. We conduct IR35 status reviews and advise on structuring.
