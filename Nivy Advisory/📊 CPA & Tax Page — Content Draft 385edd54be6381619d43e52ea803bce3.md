## The Problem with Single-Country Tax Advisors

Most accountants are brilliant within their own jurisdiction. But when your income, entity, or assets cross a border, you hit a coordination problem: your US CPA doesn't see what your UK accountant is doing, and neither of them sees the full picture.

That gap is where penalties, double-taxation exposure, and missed treaty benefits live.

Nivy Advisory was built specifically for the overlap — advising on both sides of every jurisdiction pair we work in, not handing you off to a local firm and hoping they sync.

## A Note on Cross-Border Complexity

Here's a scenario that illustrates what a single-country advisor misses:

> You're a UK LTD company director who also holds a US Green Card. You're required to file US taxes as a US person (Form 1040 + potentially FBAR/FATCA) AND UK Self Assessment AND Corporation Tax for your company. The US-UK tax treaty allows you to offset UK tax paid against your US liability — but only if the right elections are made at the right time, on the right forms. A US CPA who doesn't know the UK filing cycle, and a UK accountant who doesn't understand the US treaty elections, will each do their half correctly and still leave money on the table — or create an inadvertent double-tax position.
> 

This is the scenario Nivy Advisory exists for.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to file in both countries if I live in one but earn in another?**

In most cases, yes — and how much you owe in each depends on the tax treaty between those countries, how long you've been resident, and the nature of your income. The answer is almost never simple, which is why a cross-border review is the right first step.

**What happens if I've missed prior years' filings?**

This is more common than people admit. For US expats, the IRS Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures exist specifically for late filers. We handle catch-up filings regularly — the key is acting before the IRS or HMRC acts first.

**Can you file taxes even if you're not doing my bookkeeping?**

Yes. Tax filing and bookkeeping can be scoped separately. We'll need clean or reconciled books to file accurately — if yours need work, we'll scope a catch-up bookkeeping pass first.

**What's the difference between FBAR and FATCA?**

Both report foreign financial accounts/assets, but to different agencies, with different thresholds and forms. FBAR (FinCEN 114) goes to the Treasury; FATCA (Form 8938) goes to the IRS. Many US persons need to file both. We review both obligations as part of every US expat engagement.

**How do you handle tax authority notices and audits?**

We respond directly on your behalf. You forward the notice to us; we review, respond, and keep you informed of the outcome. Correspondence handling is included in all ongoing engagements.
