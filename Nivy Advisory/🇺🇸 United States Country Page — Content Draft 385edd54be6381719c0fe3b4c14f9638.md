## 3 Things US Expats Get Wrong — And What It Costs Them

**1. "I don't owe US tax if I live abroad."**

The US taxes based on citizenship, not residency. Every US citizen and Green Card holder must file a US tax return every year, regardless of where they live. Non-filing — even if you owe nothing — can trigger penalties and complications with passport renewal.

**2. "My foreign bank account is under $10,000, so I don't need to file FBAR."**

The FBAR threshold is $10,000 in *aggregate* across all foreign accounts at any point during the year — not per account. Three accounts with $4,000 each trigger the requirement. Missed FBAR filings carry penalties of up to $10,000 per non-willful violation, per year.

**3. "My UK / Canadian / Australian accountant handles everything."**

A non-US accountant is not licensed to advise on US tax law, and a US CPA who doesn't know your host country's rules can't optimize your treaty position or avoid double-taxation. You need both sides covered by advisors who coordinate — not two separate engagements that don't speak to each other.

## US Tax Treaties — What They Mean for You

The US has tax treaties with the UK, Canada, and Australia (among others). These treaties determine:

- Which country has the primary right to tax specific types of income
- What credits or exclusions you can claim to avoid double-taxation
- How pension income, dividends, capital gains, and real estate are treated cross-border

**Applying the treaty correctly requires:** knowing which elections to make, on which forms, by which deadline — across both countries simultaneously. This is exactly where single-country advisors create gaps.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to file US taxes if I live and work entirely outside the US?**

Yes, if you're a US citizen or Green Card holder. The US taxes based on citizenship, not residency. You must file a return every year. You may owe nothing after the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE) and Foreign Tax Credits — but you still must file.

**What's the difference between FBAR and FATCA?**

Both report foreign financial accounts and assets, but to different agencies with different thresholds and forms. FBAR (FinCEN 114) goes to the Treasury Department; FATCA (Form 8938) goes to the IRS. Thresholds differ based on filing status and residency. Many expats need to file both. We review both obligations as part of every expat engagement.

**I haven't filed US taxes for several years. What should I do?**

The IRS Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures exist specifically for US persons who have non-willfully failed to file. There are two versions: Streamlined Foreign Offshore (for expats) and Streamlined Domestic Offshore. We handle catch-up filings regularly — the key is acting before the IRS contacts you.

**Can you file both my US return and my UK (or Canadian / Australian) return?**

Yes — and filing both together, coordinated under one engagement, is where the real value is. Treaty elections, foreign tax credit timing, and cross-border income sequencing all need to be optimized together, not by two accountants who never speak.

**Does my non-US LLC or company need to file a US tax return?**

It depends on whether you have US-sourced income, US clients, or a US entity. Foreign-owned US LLCs (even single-member, disregarded) are required to file Form 5472. We assess US filing obligations as part of every international business engagement.
