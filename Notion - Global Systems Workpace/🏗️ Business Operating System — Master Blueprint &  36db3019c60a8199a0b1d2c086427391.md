---

# 📌 HOW THIS WILL BE EXECUTED — READ FIRST

This plan will be built in **4 structured phases**, each with a clear start condition, end condition, and measurable output. No phase begins until the previous one produces its proof-of-concept.

- 📋 Execution Philosophy (Click to Expand)
    
    **Why phases matter:** Starting everything at once creates chaos. Each phase is designed so a founder with limited time can execute it without burning out or burning cash.
    
    **Who executes what:** The founder handles strategy and systems. Learners handle execution tasks. Freelancers handle revenue-risk tasks. Nobody does everything.
    
    **How we prevent failure:** Every phase has explicit failure checkpoints. If those checkpoints aren't hit, we diagnose before advancing.

## DEPT 1 — Training & Learning Department

### Purpose

Convert raw, inexperienced learners into operationally useful contributors. This department is the engine that feeds all other departments.

### Why This Exists

Without a training system, every new person requires individual onboarding by the founder. This doesn't scale. A documented training system means the 50th learner gets the same quality experience as the 1st, without founder involvement.

### Learner Lifecycle (6 Stages)

**Stage 1 — Application & Filtering**

- Candidate fills Google Form
- Form asks: availability, intent statement, expected outcome, "what do you know about outreach?"
- Red flags auto-filter: vague answers, unrealistic salary expectations, zero availability
- Only serious applicants get Orientation invitation

**Stage 2 — Orientation (The Truth Session)**

- Teach: how the ecosystem works, what they will NOT get (salary, placement guarantee)
- Teach: probability-based sales math (1000 outreach → 50 replies → 10 meetings → 2 clients)
- Teach: what they WILL get (systems, exposure, portfolio, growth path)
- Goal: kill unrealistic expectations before they create problems later

**Stage 3 — Beginner Assignments (Days 1–14)**

- Task 1: Collect 20 leads in a spreadsheet with specified format
- Task 2:Create and Optimize a social media profiles(fb, linkedin twitter or any other platform where we get clients using SOP
- Task 3: Write 5 outreach messages for given persona
- Task 4: Submit daily report for 5 consecutive days
- Evaluation: Consistency + Quality + Communication

**Stage 4 — Module Training (Parallel to Assignments)**

| Module | Topic | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Industry Reality | Video (15 min) + Quiz |
| 2 | Service Selection | Video (20 min) + Task |
| 3 | Profile Optimization | Video (15 min) + Live task |
| 4 | Outreach Systems | Video (25 min) + Practice |
| 5 | Sales Psychology & Probability | Video (20 min) + Discussion |
| 6 | Operational Workflows | Video (20 min) + Reporting task |
| 7 | Practical Assignments | Mixed format |
| 8 | Advanced Growth | Unlocked after Level 1 complete |

**Stage 5 — Evaluation (End of Week 2–3)**

- Score each learner on the Performance Scorecard
- Categories: Consistency (30%), Communication (25%), Quality (25%), Speed (10%), Initiative (10%)
- Score 70%+ → Advance to Operator track
- Score 40–69% → Remedial track (extra assignments, re-evaluation)
- Score <40% → Exit (politely, with feedback)

**Stage 6 — Advancement**

- Top performers get: advanced assignments, real outreach support tasks, freelancer collaboration exposure
- Mid performers get: repeat beginner track with specific improvement targets
- All performers get: certificate at module completion

## DEPT 2 — Outreach Operations Department

### Purpose

Generate consistent, qualified lead pipelines. This is the revenue-feeding department.

### Why This Exists

Without a structured outreach system, lead generation becomes inconsistent. Freelancers waste time on bad leads. No data means no improvement. This department brings discipline to revenue-generating activities.

### 4-Layer Outreach System

**Layer 1 — Lead Research**

- Who does it: Learners (beginner tasks) + Operators (verification)
- Task: Identify target industries, collect contact data (LinkedIn, [Apollo.io](http://Apollo.io) free tier, manual research)
- Output format: Standard lead spreadsheet with Name / Company / Role / LinkedIn URL / Email / Niche / Notes
- Quality check: Operator verifies 10% sample of every batch

**Layer 2 — CRM Management**

- Tool: Notion CRM or Google Sheets (Phase 1–2), HubSpot (Phase 3+)
- Fields tracked: Lead status / Last contact date / Follow-up date / Conversation summary / Opportunity stage
- Who updates: Operators after every outreach touch
- Review cadence: Weekly audit by Team Lead

**Layer 3 — Outreach Execution**

| Channel | Who Executes | Volume Target | Key SOP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LinkedIn DM | Operators | 30–50/day | SOP-OUTREACH-001 |
| Email Outreach | Operators | 20–40/day | SOP-OUTREACH-002 |
| Follow-up Sequences | Operators + Automation | Per CRM trigger | SOP-OUTREACH-003 |
| WhatsApp (warm leads) | Freelancers | As needed | SOP-OUTREACH-004 |

**Layer 4 — Follow-Up System (The 5-Touch Sequence)**

| Touch | Timing | Format | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Touch 1 | Day 0 | Initial connection/message | Open conversation |
| Touch 2 | Day 3 | Value-add message | Build interest |
| Touch 3 | Day 7 | Soft follow-up | Re-engage |
| Touch 4 | Day 14 | Direct ask (call/meeting) | Appointment |
| Touch 5 | Day 21 | Final attempt + resource | Last chance |

> **Key Teaching:** Most closes happen at Touch 3–5. Anyone who quits at Touch 1 is leaving 80% of their pipeline on the table.
> 

### Outreach Metrics Tracked Weekly

| Metric | Tracked By | Review Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leads added to CRM | Team Lead | Daily |
| Outreach messages sent | CRM / Tracker | Daily |
| Reply rate | Operator report | Weekly |
| Meetings booked | Freelancer report | Weekly |
| Deals closed | Founder / Finance | Weekly |

## DEPT 3 — Freelancer Partnership Department

### Purpose

Manage external execution partners who handle revenue-risk tasks (sales, closing, delivery).

### Why This Exists

Freelancers absorb income uncertainty that the company cannot bear as fixed salaries. They already have the mindset. This department structures the relationship so both sides benefit without creating employment obligations.

### 3 Freelancer Categories

**Category A — Closers**

- Role: Take qualified appointments, run sales calls, close clients
- Compensation: 15–30% commission on closed deals
- Risk: High (only earn on close)
- Profile: Experienced in sales, comfortable with rejection

**Category B — Delivery Specialists**

- Role: Execute client work after closing (bookkeeping, outreach service, VA work, digital marketing)
- Compensation: Project-based or hourly share
- Risk: Medium (earn on delivery completion)
- Profile: Skilled executors, reliable, communicative

**Category C — Mentors / Reviewers**

- Role: Review learner work, guide operators, improve workflow quality
- Compensation: Per-batch fee OR revenue share on learners they advance
- Risk: Low
- Profile: Experienced operators willing to teach

### Freelancer Onboarding Process (4 Steps)

**Step 1 — Application & Screening**

- Form collects: portfolio, niche, experience, availability, expected commission structure, attitude to rejection
- Red flags: expecting fixed salary, unwilling to use CRM, can't articulate their last deal

**Step 2 — System Training (1 session, 45 minutes)**

- Teach: CRM usage, reporting standards, communication protocol, escalation rules
- Provide: Freelancer Handbook (5-page PDF), Revenue Share Agreement template
- Requirement: Must sign agreement before any client access

**Step 3 — Controlled Start**

- First 2 weeks: Work on 1–2 leads only (supervised)
- All communication via shared channels (no private client contact)
- Daily brief update required

**Step 4 — Full Collaboration**

- After 2 clean weeks: Full lead access
- Weekly performance review
- Quarterly renegotiation of terms if desired

### Freelancer Risk Control Systems

| Risk | Prevention System |
| --- | --- |
| Client poaching | All initial contact via company channels. NDAs for senior freelancers. |
| Ghosting | 2-miss rule: 2 missed updates = warning, 3rd = suspension |
| Inconsistency | Weekly scorecard review — consistent underperformance triggers conversation |
| Ego conflicts | Written communication norms in handbook. Founder escalation path documented. |
| Revenue disputes | All deals tracked in shared CRM. Invoices go through founder. |

## DEPT 4 — Institute Partnership Department

### Purpose

Create a self-replenishing pipeline of motivated learners through institutional relationships.

### Why This Exists

Hiring 1-by-1 from job portals is slow and inconsistent. Institutes provide batches of motivated, already-paying students who want practical exposure. One institute relationship = 10–30 learners per cohort with zero recruitment cost.

### Institute Partnership Funnel (5 Steps)

**Step 1 — Identify & Qualify Institutes**

Target types:

- Digital Marketing Institutes
- Accounting / Tally / GST Institutes
- Freelancing / Skill Development Centers
- MBA/BBA Colleges with placement gaps
- Online coaching platforms

Qualification criteria:

- Active student base (50+ students/year)
- Has a placement or practical training gap
- Director is accessible and growth-oriented

**Step 2 — Partnership Pitch**

Core message: *"We give your students real industry workflow experience — not just certificates."*

Pitch structure (15 minutes max):

1. Their problem: students graduate without practical skills
2. Your solution: live execution tasks, SOPs, CRM, outreach experience
3. What students get: portfolio, exposure, progression path
4. What institute gets: stronger placement positioning, differentiation
5. What we need: access to batch + 2 sessions/month

**Step 3 — Pilot Program**

- 1 free workshop (1.5 hours): teach outreach math + probability
- Practical challenge: 20 leads in 48 hours
- Identify top 5 performers from pilot
- Present results to institute director

**Step 4 — Formal Partnership**

- Sign simple MOU (not contract)
- Define: session frequency, batch size, student selection criteria
- Provide: co-branded certificate template, institute-specific progress report format

**Step 5 — Ongoing Integration**

- Monthly report to institute on student progress
- Quarterly review meeting with director
- Annual renewal conversation

### What Institutes Should NEVER Be Promised

> ❌ Guaranteed placements
> 

> ❌ Guaranteed salaries
> 

> ❌ Income numbers
> 

> ❌ "Your students will get jobs"
> 

### What Institutes ARE Promised

> ✅ Practical execution exposure
> 

> ✅ Real industry workflows
> 

> ✅ Portfolio building for students
> 

> ✅ Structured training modules
> 

> ✅ Progress reports and certificates
